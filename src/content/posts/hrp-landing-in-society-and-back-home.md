I am so happy and overwhelmed by all the things and all the people that happened to me during the 7 weeks of walking in July and August, it is hard for me to process, even harder to spell it out in words. But it seems like writing things down / writing about it is a good way to process things. So here we go, take it or leave it. This Blog is mostly for my own processing of events that touched me, and this one did touch me in a lot of different ways.

1st I have to say, that I expected this trip to be physically hard and long, I prepared for it for at least 9 months and felt pretty good about my conditioning. Maybe even to the point that I felt a little over-prepared. How wrong was I??!! I also considered this 7 weeks trip to be another level of mental challenge. And that part was 100% spot on. The HRP was not only longer, but longer in every day **km** walked and meters of elevation **climbed** and **descended**, but most significantly, it proved challenging when looking at the **type of trail surface** we needed to move over. This is an effect I knew about of course, but found hard to assess before starting the day's route. Some days we only walked 7km, but it took us 6 hours to get this done and we were really tired mentally and physically. Other days, with similar gradients up and down, could have 25km and we would manage this day with relative ease in 6-7 hours. Hiking 7-11 hours a day, day after day played tricks with my mind. We had the whole day, tried to be efficient with our use of time, and still ended up falling short of some of the things we needed to do in our time AFTER hiking. My mind was focused more and more on only the things that are essentially needed for the next task at hand. Things like washing sox, walking shirt, shorts; seeing the rest of the gear in working order through sewing, patching, gluing, ..... keeping your body limber and ready for the next day, massaging parts that needed help, cooling parts that might cause trouble the next few days through inflammation or other symptoms of overuse, massaging feet, legs shoulders, .... all that between arriving at camp from a long day of hiking and before dinner had to be cooked or we could get it from the Refugio. Sounds like a lot? YES. That was only to take care of the body. What do I do to this mind of mine? Isolate myself? Listen to music? Meditate? Talk to others some? Sleep? Do you even have time for this or should I better use the 10 min before dinner to take some amazing Pictures of this area we are in? It seems to me now, that life was reduced to a minimum of absolute necessities. This is fine with me, I am a simple-minded guy, I don't need a lot of the fluff of daily life that is offered on many city street corners, shops, malls, and online. 

I want to share one event of one night about 8 days before the finish. I usually sleep like a rock, fall asleep fast and only wake up to get up and start the day. This one day, I could not fall asleep. I was not calm at all and I felt a lot of emotions coming from my heart area. I never felt that way before, I did not understand what was going on and did not know if I needed to be worried about it or not. But I was not worried about this, just super interested and curious. After I sat up straight in our tent for several minutes, not knowing what to do, Ryan woke up next to me. Good, I thought, someone I can talk to about this and he will listen and try to understand. So we talked, and finally, I told him that most likely Ineeded a hug. A good one, let's see if this does something. Oh boy. Did it do something!! I started crying for a few minutes and I could feel how much it helped. So we hugged some more. If anyone here knows Ryan, he is a World-Class hugger. When I stopped crying we talked some more about what all that could be about and finally, I decided that it should be ok to try and sleep now. Hand in hand, we fell asleep and I slept like a rock. What an experience!! Analysis with a number of different people made it pretty clear to me that it must have been the tension of organizing it all, thepressure of every day to do what we did, and now the relief that we only had another 7-8 days to walk and we would be done. No more major obstacles to overcome. We had a pretty good shot at completing this mission. All this mixed up in my chest and mind made it necessary to cry those tears of relief and let all this come out of me.

I had another, much more mellow but longer effect waiting for me at the days of "re-entry" into regular life. It was a bigger step than I expected, and I did not feel that when I finished the Trans-Alp 2 years ago.

## Re-entry

After our last day of hiking, in the heat, in the strong wind, downhill for what seemed forever, the last 2 hours of what seemed to be walking into the stream of a hot hairdryer; the effect did not show yet for me. Emotions took the driver seat and relief, happiness was the dominating emotion. Even when we ate our leftover food near the beach, with no issues, finding a hotel room ... dinner, and sleeping breakfast all failed to show a big impact on me. But arriving in Barcelona changed everything. I felt completely lost. I was at a loss of meaningful thoughts for the situation and without any idea of what to do, or what to do about it. After a small beer Ryan and I sat on a rental bike to see some sites. We did not seem to find what we were after. We decided to lock the bikes and eat some overpriced tourist foods and a BIG beer. I remember looking at the people passing by on this Sunday afternoon and many thoughts and questions came to mind. These people seemed so "conditioned" so much living a "shell life" Some seemed truly empty, without purpose or reason. I think insome ways, coming from where I came from, this was very much life without purpose. Why are you walking around with this pretty dress / fancy shirt, make-up, handbag, ..... Why aren't you doing something productive to your goal of today?How can you NOT have a goal today?

These are just the thoughts I remember. I am sure there was much more going on in my head at the time. I also don't want to create the impression I judged any of these people in any way. These were just thoughts of this time, of this state of mind of mine, coming from the 7 weeks of hiking I did. Just a little snapshot I wanted to recall because I think this might not come much more often in my life. It reminds me a little of what I believe the movie "[Poor Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Things_\(film\))" wanted to highlight, among many other things. Society conditions us all. 

The fact that Muriel picked me up from the Airport in Florence was awesome. I truly missed her. And it is good. The first hour or so even Muriel seemed a little like a stranger to my observations. But that passed quickly. Driving in the passenger seat in Florence was also interestingly exciting to me. I was glad not to drive myself. Even in the morning, I preferred Muriel to drive the treacherous street of Florence to the Airport. She flew out that afternoon to London.

Arriving home, first seeing our Casentino Valley from the top filled me with a lot of joy and happiness. When coming home, it all was familiar in some kind of way, but it still felt foreign and strange. That never happened to me before.

All this passed on in the next week or so, and the 29km (1400m Climb) mountain race in our area helped me do something I knew very well. I hiked almost all of the distance. I could not, and could not be bothered to run very long. SOOO much beauty to see. Why would I rush through it? I still managed to not be the last one crossing the finish line for this distance though. I managed to complete this course in under 4 1/2 hours which told me clearly again, in good hiking shape I am. Long legs I have and I trained 7 weeks to use them well.
