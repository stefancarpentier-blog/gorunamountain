So I made it from Nuernberg to the Alps in 3 days in ~35-41C. That was a little nuts in retrospect. It taxed my body a lot, but I made it. I met Klaus in Landeck, Austria. It rained that afternoon and evening, we went for an Italian dinner and had great talks between the two of us. I tried local Austrian Tiramisu and I have to say, it was really good. The chef prided himself with making it all from scratch. The portion was more meant for 3 people, but I did not care and ate it all. Klaus and I met up in the morning, not too early as the weather did not demand it. I quickly felt my legs, not in a good way. Clearly the last days demanded their toll. We managed to take the scenic, less-traveled route up to Reschenpass. ==> 5km more and about an extra 100-200m of climbing. But the benefit was a whole lot fewer cars and riding in 3 countries in an hour or so. We came up to almost 1600m in great weather. We followed the "Via Claudia Augusta" path for some sections. I followed this path for a lot of km already days before. Going up Fernpass it was a true blessing to have it as the alternative, riding on the main road" now is a stinky mess and borderline dangerous. It's steep, gravel road and you need to push at least one section of Fernpass. On Reschenpass it is much more ridable, even with skinnier tires. Here are some details of the route built in ~15BC-47AD by the Romans, you guessed it, the son of [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus). [LINK to the road](http://www.viaclaudia.org/it/via-claudia-augusta.html).

\

Klaus turned around back down the pass as the weather was supposed to turn for the worse in a hurry that day. And he got wet on the way down, soaking wet.

![DSC01236-S](/photos/dsc01236-s.jpg)

Not for me though, I tried to recover my weak body with some lunch and then ride along the really stunning Reschensee with hundreds of Kite surfers jumping about. Quiet the scene. The way down the Italian side of the pass was a TRUE treat. The people of South Tirol created a jewel of a cycle path. Completely separate from the road for the cars, they established a safe and truly enjoyable pass experience. What a treat. I was not here at least 25-30 years. The difference is amazing. It is so well established and safe, I found myself wondering when the 1st rest station just for Bikes would show up. But oops, there it was already. Complete with on-ramp and parking, self-service or catered. I was blown away. And on top of it all, the next 150km I passed more apples than I think I ever did. Fields and fields without end full with Apples.

![d764ceffb791fa0ced41db2bf9d61b91d29f5f57592b8be6227c1b00ede47206](/photos/img_20190729_112754806_hdr-s.jpg)

I held a rest day the next day and only rolled down to Milano that day (32km) it rained on and off but it was just what I needed. I managed to get a very interesting accommodation in Merano. [Castel Pienzenau](http://www.pienzenau.com/#pic/thumbs/schloss_0.jpg). A little gem on the outskirts of Meran towards the hills. It has private rooms that looked quite stylish and I was the only one in a Dorm of up to 6 people. Any time I can, I would like to stay there again.

The following day was one of tailwind coupled with a slow and long downhill on a supercycle path. It seemed like the whole world is on bikes, racers young and old, families, couples - fast and slow, e-bikes and traditional. The weather could not have been any better. The Sun is out and the temperatures are very reasonable. I had 95km down at lunchtime in Trento and only started at 9.00. The only climb of the day was a real one, going up to Lago di Caldonazzo. A tip I got from Kurt. It is one of his favorites in the Alps it seems. Simple and beautiful. People and things seem to be in order here. A community centered on tourists and trying to take care of them the best way they know. I slept in a tiny tiny BnB Casa D Arte, run by a Russian family from Moscow. The young daughter let me in and we quickly had an interesting conversation going. What a delight to talk to. Open, friendly, knows rules and knows to appreciate. On the other side the room was decorated in a really old and kitschy style, the stuff that was on the breakfast table the next morning must have been the worst attempt at breakfast for me, maybe ever. EVERYTHING was wrapped in Plastic. The attempt at a Pain O Chocolate, plastic, the cookies (3 types), the jam, the yogurt..... No bread. Oh, there was coffee and it came out of a Bialetti type machine, no plastic. I did not know what to eat and just walked out, no food. The following 70-80km prooved to be one amazingly scenic set. Valstagna is one beautiful place on earth. Go there and ride it down, not up. Or either way, ride it but bring a set of GPX files to make sure you find all the nifty little intersections. I managed NOT to find one and decided one exit on a "**car only**" street could not be so bad. I just could not find where else to go. I was in for a scary experience. ~2.5km of two-lane Freeway (Autobahn) style road, with Italian style narrow lanes, no emergency lane and big Semi-trucks passing me. On the right side, 2-meter high guard rails made sure I can't just escape the misery. Stefan happy to be off, was not happy with his last decision at all!!! The valley is really worth a journey. Here some details [LINK.](https://www.lifeintravel.it/en/valsugana-cycle-path-from-pergine-to-bassano-del-grappa-by-bicycle.html) The second 1/2 of the day was a slightly different story. Like crossing a line, when I rolled out of the Alps, then there was flat. Long and wide, flat. The Poo Delta. It's moist, hot and wide just as it is long. Think about the CA central valley, just with water and humidity. After lunch in Bassano del Grappa, I headed for this THING. It surely made sure the reputation is kept up. Truck traffic on pretty small roads. I tried to escape on others, sometimes with success, sometimes without. 2/3rds into the 55km stretch, I finally found a cool Oasis. A pub and I got more than 2 liters of water into me, without a flinch. By the time I arrived in Padova, my decision was sealed. I am taking the train for the next day's distance. Padova seemed like a traffic hub of the area. I did not really find anything that sparked my interest. But to be fair, I did not look very long. I just needed some food and needed to be out of the sun.

The next morning I rode the Station and bought a E 18.00 ticket to Forli, for me and my bike. I met two Italian riders on the platform and we hooked up for the train ride. A really nice French-Italian couple that just set out on their bike trip to Greece. Lea and Filippo. They seem to know how to live and know about travel, plan less ==> experience more. Out of all that I also heard a recent and locally pretty embarrassing story for an Italian city they traveled through [LINK .](https://www.repubblica.it/cronaca/2019/07/10/news/_niente_acqua_andate_via_la_parrocchia_di_restena_caccia_i_turisti_assetati-230890270/?refresh_ce)

# "No water, go away", the parishioners drive out thirsty tourists. [LINK](https://www.repubblica.it/cronaca/2019/07/10/news/_niente_acqua_andate_via_la_parrocchia_di_restena_caccia_i_turisti_assetati-230890270/?refresh_ce)

They were denied water in a little town. As a cyclist, I cannot understand what can cause this type of behavior. As a resident of the current USA and Mr "T"s hatred of anything and anyone that does not look like you, I sadly can. Read this article and the impact the two made by getting it published. Bravo!! This is the only way to fight ignorance and fobia. Thank you.

**Forli**, not the center of the universe, but it is an old place, kept up well, in the area of the Adriatic coast and also in the are of the Poo Delta. ==> It is really hot and humid in the summer, not cooling off much at night. The little cool off there is, happens only really after 21.00 h. I found it interesting to feel the pulse of a place, at least a little. For me, it starts with how people live their lives, where they do and when they do it. Here, the town was pretty much on slow motion since the time I arrived. Just after noontime. This is a climate where SIESTA makes sense. I have no idea where it was invented, but I can imagine it was in a place with similar climate than this. You don't want to go out on the street from 11.30 till maybe 16.00 . Forget about socializing before 21.00 . Chasing young girls, hanging out with friends from the city, leading a social life seems to start again at around 21.00 . It makes total sense. I caught the starting part of it all as I needed to get to bed for my early rise and start of the ride at ~ 6.00 if I want to avoid the intense midday heat.
