### Flamkuche vs. Tarte Flambe - Don't mix it up

What is that anyway? Flames? cake? Tarte? What is all this about. It’s just a Pizza you say, a little thinner. No no, you are stepping into dangerous waters, my friend. You enter the Area of Alsace and you don’t mess around with that.

For the un-inclined, I am talking about a long tradition of making something delicious out of dough, onions, olive oil and some yogurt mixture. You can call it related to a pizza but of course, it is not a Pizza.

1. it is of rectangular shape
    
2. It is big
    
3. It is paper thin (I am not kidding)
    
4. There is (almost) nothing on it
    
5. You can eat 2 or 3 of them and will live to talk about it
    
6. Today you can get them savory (usual) and sweet as a dessert style
    
7. Then need to be made in a wood-fired oven
    
8. 2 minutes and they are done
    

I had the opportunity to try some of these beauties during our stay with Frederique and Jean-Loup in Barr, just south of Strasbourg. They have built themselves an amazing home 15 years ago, made exclusively from concrete. Steel and concrete, no wallpaper and very little else that would disrupt your impression of this well planned and amazingly beautiful home. Jean-Loup managed to place a few decorative plants in the forms before the concrete was poured. The result is the Relief of the plant on the face of the concrete, sometimes the stem or part of the leaves remain in place. Then some leaves are lightly painted to accentuate the form. Awesome. Even the light fixtures are concrete and they are pretty, provide great lighting and look super modern.

HOUSE PICTURES

Back to the Tarte Flambe!! Frederique and Jean-Loup brought us to a party where the local Tarte Flambe master showed off his skill. Christian, he comes from the area and comes from a bakers family. If I understood right, he thinks his Dad brought the Tarte Flambe from an area a few km south to Barr. He worked there for a while, learned the skill of Tarte Flambe and then moved, brought along the art and skillset. Christian makes Tarte Flambe for the last 40 years in different settings. whether it is from home or in a commercial kitchen or like now our to his mobile, wood-fired oven. He set it up on a trailer and brings all her needs to serve 20 or 100 people with what they want. **Tarte Flambe.**

[https://www.vegetaria.at/flammkuchen-ein-experiment-tarte-flambee-an-experiment/](https://www.vegetaria.at/flammkuchen-ein-experiment-tarte-flambee-an-experiment/)

GUESS WHAT? I ended up talking to Christian a bunch over the course of the night and he is a great great guy. He works hard for sure, and he is funny and knows a lot about local history and Tarte Flambe.

After listening to him I started reading a bunch of Blogs and websites and I think the difference is more regional than actual. Not to play it down, maybe I am wrong but that is what I read so far. Needless to say, I will have to try to make some of these beauties. Stay tuned.  Go to http://www.tartes-flambees.fr/ for the real deal.

### How accidents are handled in France

We were picked up by our friends Natalie and Francois and went into Strasbourg proper now. Amazing and fun to see them again, after they visited us 6 years ago in California. We dropped the bags, had a little Apero (appetizers) and went into town by bike after Emmanuel came off the TGV from Paris to join us all in the fun. Family and friends, awesome. Natalie showed us around and the weather was super warm and sunny. Strasbourg is a small and really pretty town, with a great old town center that is an island embraced on all sides by the river Ile. It is the capital of Europe. I found it a great mixture between German and French there. Exquisite French food, German structure. We rode into town, took pictures and Oooops, Emmanuel managed to turn his bike a little fast and a lady on her electric scooter came shooting along the bike path. The result was a scraped up knee, a hard fall on the back on the head on the ground. She did not move much at first, but it was soon clear that she was ok in the grand scheme. Having lived in the US for 23 years, I was amazed about the ease this case was handled. Natalie called the Ambulance, I managed the bike and pedestrian traffic and when the helpers arrived (a little slow I must say), there was very little fuzz given to who did what and no police was present anyway. 10 minutes in the van for a quick checkup and an exchange of email addresses is all it took to get her on the way to X-Rays and us back on our way into town. No insurance and no anxiety about the dramatic expense this all will cause. Amazing what a healthcare system can do to a population. Take one more worry out of your hair and you can actually live. Why dod the US citizen prefer to struggle so much with the concept of a population that is cared for when it comes to health. I am in awe and will never understand.

### Breakfast in Strasbourg, plates ????

It's just a little thing, but I liked it. We were so perfectly hosted at Natalie’s and Francois’ apartment, I was amazed and still, I am in debt. Thank you. There always is a laugh, a little something to eat and a little something to talk about. There is no rush to get anywhere and harmony seems to be on top of the list. For breakfast, there were pastries, of course, coffee and tea, fruit and juices. But there were no plates. Just a napkin and the table. That is all I needed, but just so unusual for me not to have a plate. Cute.

**And now we are off to Berlin**. Two train rides and close to 800 km distance. Less than 7 hours and we will be there, and no driving needed. I love it. Berlin, here we come.

Thanks, Chris for giving us so many handy tips on what to do and where to go there. We are looking forward to seeing Isabel again. She will be there too and we will tag along the former native “_Berliner_”.
