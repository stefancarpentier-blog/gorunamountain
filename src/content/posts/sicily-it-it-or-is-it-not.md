I mean, **is it or is it not** part of Italy? I personally did not know what to expect and went on to think I will see just another, a different part of Italy. Well, I did. So different to me that I have to scratch my head a few times. Parts of Sicily are much closer to Tunis that they are to Rome, just speaking geographically. We saw signs on houses, in Arabic ![DSC02227-S](/photos/dsc02227-s.jpg)writing. I saw people whos facial features much closer resemble ones I expect to see in the African continent that here. I think it is another clear evidence races, looks and ways of life don't follow borders but follow geographies and connections between people. I find that fascinating to see and discover.

On our way from the West to the East side of Sicily, we made our way through some serious agricultural areas. Not at all scenic, the driving was hectic, pushy and really tiring. The valley of the temples right outside of Agrigento was truly worth a visit.

\[caption id="attachment\_1491" align="alignright" width="361"\]![DSC02301-S](/photos/dsc02301-s.jpg) Greek temple with a Church resembling modification inside.\[/caption\]

Here history does not seem to stop at Year 0, Jesus birth. Here you see the temples and clear evidence of what Greek people did here to expand their territories. Even though some temples were built way before Year 0, it had to change the temple look to something resembling the qualities of a church or be destroyed.

 

 

We stayed in Comiso for the night and had a nice interaction with an Italian man, well into retirement age. He worked at VW plant in Wolfsburg for 30 years and praised the hell out of

![IMG\_20190913\_190836-S](/photos/img_20190913_190836-s.jpg)

Germany. He now gets his retirement payments from Germany and has a good life as it seems. Still, he is worried a lot about his two sons, they try to make it work here in Sicily and he has little confidence it will work out well for them. He and his old friend were a cute Duo.

 

A trip to the winery Occhipinti was on our list of things to check out. an organic winemaker that is up an coming. I found most interesting that the same grape type in an almost identical area (200-500m apart), following the identical winemaking processes,  can deliver such a different taste in wine. I did not like even one of their wines, but that was "almost" besides the point.

We spent 4 days in Syracuse, a pretty wealthy city on the east coast of Sicily. Noone less than Archimedes was born here. I am far from a historian, but this man did some impressive feats more than 2000 years ago (287BC). ![Archi](/photos/archi.png)You might recall a Physics lecture where you tried to determine the volume of an unregular shaped object by immersing it in water and calculating the water level difference before and after. The Archimedes Principle.

 

 

 

I went on a few bike rides with my little Brompton and found some amazing beauty, but had to pay the price of enduring super bright sun and hot hot temperatures. I can only recommend the peninsula south of the city (  ) as well as the area around "_Necropolis of Pantalica_**"**
