Yesterday I went on one of my Unicycle rides, alone, this time in the Oakland hills in the Chabot Park area. Clearly, I am a man, I am tall and I probably come across as confident. I don't think I come across as dominant or threatening or as a womanizer. I am sitting on a UNIcycle that I would expect to put me a little out of the norm of the general threatening male parties, in the eyes of a woman; I think, I hope that is true. So I am on my way for the short ride I have in mind and I happen to meet two different women along the trail, both walking their dogs. The fact that really shocked me was the impression I got from both of them as I come closer. I clearly sensed "I am scared of you" what will you do in the next 10seconds, do I need to defend myself? and more and more. I ended up quickly asking one of the two women for the way, trying my best not to look at her too long, stating I am almost on my way back and looking away as much as I found reasonable. And the impression did not change much at all, I don't think I made the feeling any better, I am very sorry. [![](/photos/image1.png)](/photos/image1.png)

One my way back up the hill, I had enough time to think about what just happened, what situation I was in, what impressions I might have caused. It really made me feel very sad and it brought home to me one more time how women have to look at their direct environment, at people and the street ahead of them, at people they might encounter or cross paths with in the next few minutes. I pretty much never have to do this and used to take this for granted and in the last few years more and more had to realize how different my view of the world is, and how lucky I am to be born as a buy a man and also white. I cannot see how I could have behaved differently to make the situation easier for both of these women, it seems to be an ingrained and well justified fear of anything male in the situation we met yesterday. My need to FIX this problem comes to the front of my mind. What can we do to de-fuse this situation for all these women. what can women do to get this out of the way, what can I do to get this out of the way ...... I see the only promising way is to grab the situation by the root. The root of the problem I see as being the man, the mental state and trained behaviour men receive as they grow up and is seen as "normal". The male dominance physically as well as in general their place in society. How the man fits into society in comparison to a woman, patriarchal or matriarchal society behaviour is the key word I want to question, question how we bring up males next to females and what results we receive right now. How can we change this upbringing to receive respectful men that women don't have to fear every minute of almost every day, just walking in a park, alone. I believe we cannot expect significant change if we try to "empower women" ; they are the victims and changing the equation between the two this way will only make the problem a little less bad, the consequences maybe less severe for each encounter between man and women. I truly believe we need to address the problem at the source, address the male behaviour pattern. But HOW do we address this the best? I don't think I am competent for this, but I want to be sure we actually work on the source of the problem. Work on changing male thought pattern when it comes to women, when it comes to expectations to women, when it comes to valuing women in our life.
