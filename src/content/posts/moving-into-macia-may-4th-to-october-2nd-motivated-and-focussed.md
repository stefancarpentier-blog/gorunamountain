It's been 2 months since the last update on the house and us in Tuscany. It was hard and focussed, it was taxing and then some more. But finally we moved in. YES, we can barely believe it ourself. October 2nd was the first night we spent in the house. It is not finished, but it is livable. We have hot water, we have a pellet heater for the kitchen / living room, we have floor covering in all rooms, we have all bathrooms operational (but shower cabins are strangely absent). We also had a few doors in the house at the time, now we have them all. I can't tell you how good it feels to sleep in your own bed, to eat and prepare food in a kitchen that is not tiny and have a fridge that is big enough so you can actually store stuff in. Going though your day knowing this is not your home. It's stable, will not change, and you have influence in what get's done. The word **HOME** certainly filled with new and important meaning for us two. It is really incredible the amount of tasks there are to make a home. I am also eternally grateful to the construction team for pulling off the project to schedule. In a house of this age, there are so many unknowns and so many surprises to deal with. Still the initial schedule of 3 1/2 months was held, and we moved in. YEPEEEEEE

In August Muriel decided it would be the best for her to eject herself from the project and visit family in Paris for a month. Just before that, Cassandre and Gabriele came to visit us, which was a huge treat!!. Then, to top it off, Sabrina and her boyfriend Leonardo came see us just before she left. Then, I had a little more time for myself as it is traditionally the month where Italians go on vacation. Though COVID sent everyone in a shut down in the Spring and start of the summer, but Italians cannot and will not miss out of their summer beach time. With or without COVID. I managed to build a Tuscan stone wall with Claudio, the lead builder on the project. He really built it and I was more a helper. But I learned a lot and was not only useless. It will frame the area of the future wooden hot tub. It took 4 a little more than 1/2 days to build it. The heat was just too intense to continue past 13.00 or 14.00. I am so so proud of the fact that I was part of it.

August also started the time of harvest. I made a lot of Blackberry jam, all straight from the property. Interestingly they looked a little dull and small during the hot and dry times of August. They tasted intense though. After a good Tuscan rain, they looked shiny, plump and glorious. So I went out for another picking session. The result was a little more diluted taste. Still good, but less intense. They were cooked and made to glorious jam anyway. Then there were yellow plums, purple plums, pears..... So good, so rich. It felt rushed to squeeze in jam making sessions after a full day of work at the house, but it also felt good to do something for the next year, plan ahead and be certain it will actually happen for a change. Later in September, we got to harvest Hazelnuts and walnuts. So many, I had to give up for this year to try and harvest them all.

![](/photos/dsc05823-s.jpg)

I also started building the kitchen at the start of September. It is not an easy task and I had more trouble making progress just because every day would start with a talk with the workers, asking questions, answering and making decisions, and and and. Then after lunch I would have an hour or two to work on that kitchen only to be more interrupted by questions. Only once all the workers left the site was I able to start some focussed kitchen work. Everything had to be super stable and even as the Marble countertop will be very heavy on the cabinets and would not tolerate any wiggling of give. Uneven floors and walls and all the rest of it did not help make this easy. but now, now I am finally done with it and wait for the delivery of the Marble. It is ready since Friday, I plan to pick it up and install next week. In the mean time we have OSB boards as countertops.

![](/photos/pxl_20200929_112803496-s.jpg)

Outdoors on the property we made huge progress through hiring a farmer and two of his big machines. a big tractor with a mowing device that seems indestructible to me, and an excavator with an even more rugged weed/bush/tree eater as attachment. Two people were working for 4 days, 10 hours a day. Now the property tot he hill side is no longer recognizable. It is like night and day. The terrace walls are visible, the blackberry bushes are gone (for now) and we can actually see what we have. It used to be like a jungle.

![](/photos/img_20200916_141040.jpg)

We are so overworked and so tired and still now and finally so happy. We can really not understand what happened the last months. We finally have a home.

Don't get me wrong, there is so much more to do and to work on and to build and to figure out and and and. This project will go on till at least January. But we have a home.

Just about a week ago, Manu and Marine came to visit and were the 1st to actually leep with us at the house. They brought small but BIG precents with them. Two young kittens they had rescued ~1month before in France, now relocated them to Italy to stay with us. SUPER TREAT.
