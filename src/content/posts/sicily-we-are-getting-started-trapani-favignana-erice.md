While the house purchase is not complete, we are starting our trip through Sicily the day after my birthday. The French are back home, we are back on the road.

What we heard, it will not really matter that we don't speak Italian here, almost no one in Sicily does either. Siculo (sìculu) seems to be a really different language, it even sounds different to the casual listener (not understanding a word). "_It the oldest of the Romance **languages derived** from Latin, and it's spoken in Sicily and in parts of southern Italy such as Reggio di Calabria and southern Puglia."_ Sicily seems to have received influence from Africa just as well as from Europe. We visit little cities here that certainly have an Arab feel to it. This will be interesting, in much more than just a beauty kind of way.

The landscape here is really really dry. Water seems to be a premium here and it shows. We see much more cactus and succulents here, but many areas are just barren and sometimes plain dirt. The coastline is truly stunning in so many places, the water is turquoise and blue and ...... amazing colors and shapes unite. We had lunch at a really well designed and placed [Agritourismo](https://goo.gl/maps/PpYdAUupUriZMU447) in Scopello. The Swiming hole close by gave us the first view of what is to come here.

\

The drive to Trapani, our next stop was nothing special, Trapani is an industrial town with a niceish Centro Storico. The next day we slept in big time and got out of bed at about 10.30. Coffee and we had a look at the port for our trip to Favignana, an island just off the coast here. As it goes when traveling, we found ourself on the next boat 15 minutes later with no bag, no toothbrush or anything to prepare for the next two days on the island. Well, here we come. We used the 10 min wait to book a room on the island and the rest was history. It was a good decision to go and it was truly beautiful there. I was amazed by the fact that most of the roads in and out of town seemed "elevated". What are all these holes for, why are they so structured and straight? We eventually learned it is the TUFF mines on the island. It is a type of stone that was apparently mined already at the times of the Romans.

![DSC01981-S](/photos/dsc01981-s.jpg)

The afternoon we dedicated to the southern part of the island and its beautiful coastline. On bikes and ready to stop any minute we were off. I think we dipped 3 times and saw so many beautiful spots. Dramatic, interesting, smooth, beautiful. As you wish, as we liked it.

The town was still busy with tourists, not crazy, but busy. I wonder what it looks like in August, the Italian tourist month. People are personable and nice, the town is cute and the food needs to be seafood. So good, so different. the 2nd day we rented a little scooter and took off to the northern part of the island. The coastline is not as dramatic as the island does not have the same cliff style drop off like the south. Still super nice and it seemed even fewer people. Our chosen lunch spot was a disappointment as they refused to serve us afer guiding us to a table. Very strange! At the backup place we received a similar, not so warm welcome, but actually got food and a great view. We took the last boat back to Sicily and found ourself touching our arms as they were sensitive from all the sun exposure the last two days. And a toothbrush was next on our agenda. NICE.

We went to a concert in the old Tuna factory in Favignana. A really great experience. We learned a lot about the island there and listened to young people making good music. The factory was part of several movies, mainly Italian language. One of them, "[The Italians](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt2875018/plotsummary?ref_=tt_ov_pl)",  is in English and draws a passionate and sensitive picture of Italy and the people. The fashion segment of it is on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WQ3VsAKYU-A).

\

A family used to own the complete island, together with 2 others. The [Florio](https://www.mareandmoretour.it/eng/highlights/florio-family-tonnara-9) family who was very successful in business at the time used to own all three of them, completely.

**Erice** was on our agenda next. It's a little town 750m above Trapani on the hill. A charming town with so much history and ancient buildings. This mixed with a few clouds coming in and out made for really great views. Italy is truly oozing with history, where ever you look, where ever you go. I know so many people say this, but experiencing it is a different story. We treated ourself to local pizza from the place 50m down the street from our stay. **SO** crowded, so crazy, so good. Not a thin crust, but still a good pizza.
