After many months of planning, literally thousands of e mails and a few gave to face meetings with the team 14 guys made their way to Golden BC. By car, by plane, stopping en route as part of his winter skiing extravaganza. Most of us had one or two warm up days, skiing at kicking horse ski resort right outside of Golden. What a treat that was. Small, personal and amazing; the resort really has only two usable lifts, but the terrain, runs and possibilities are almost endless, while the crowd are really really small. Many of the runs can only be accessed by short hikes, the others go from regular groomer runs to rope rappels through rocks and trees to get to the actual decent of choice. We where welcome to what locals described as the best day of skiing in several years. Snow and weather as well as visibility where good. There was a freestyle ski competition the weekend before and we were treated to a couple amazing lines being skied in front of our eyes. Canadian audience was in no way shy to respect the Achievent with a complete lift line applause. We adjusted to the much lighter snow and had a great day. The to of my list was skiing the T1 as one of the 1st 20 people after the recent storms. I heard from two group members "best run  **EVER** ". On the way back to the lift I managed to slowly fall on a small tree stump and bruised my ribs. Not bad, but I will get rimming of this little fall for a number of weeks, every time I bend to close my ski boots or twist. Apres ski 🎿 it was easy to link up the team with the needed supply of"organic gardening" products, in the so so strict Canada. Canadians are so friendly, fulfilling common expectations.

Six more Californians joined us that evening and so we only missed 3 more for our departure Saturday to the helicopter flight to our but and home for the next week. Sunrise Chalet of GAH, Golden Alpine Holidays. All food had to be packed into big banana boxes and taped securely. The vans FULLY loaded with gear and people we heard to the launch pad about 45min West of Golden. My 1st time being this close to a helicopter and then actually flying with it was an amazing experience. I did not know what to feel when it took off. Thrilling to say the very least.  On less than 10 minutes flight we already touched down on the snow helicopter of next to our hut. The precision of these machines is amazing people are sitting within one meet left and right of the landing gear and the pilot touches down right on the middle with complete confidence.

The hut is at 2100m elevation and send to be the hut with the biggest selection of mountains, aspects, elevation, terrain and runs. We had the great fortune to get Brad as our custodian on the hut. He actually built this and the other 3 GAH huts in the 1980ties. He has guided people for 30 years in these mountains and is a hell of a guy to have around. Especially in the snow conditions we where facing. Right from the start the avalanche danger was up on#4 of 5. After unloading we received our safety speech and went humbly on our 1st route.

Wet snow fell on a complex snow base. We choose the safest of save routes, Toro, Brian and me. South facing, low angle, on the trees. We where safe but what we heard during the 1st climb back up was nothing short of a firework. Or team mates on the south side saw one of them being released just above them. Nothing happened other than all of us ended up back in the hut with the tail between our legs and hugely humbled. 4th of July fireworks in Canada in March. WOW!
