> It is now almost six years

since we moved to Italy. I was always pretty sure we would have a good time here, with the weather, with the food, with the people. Somehow, I did not really have any doubt; I was never worried. But for people who know me, that might not be such a big surprise. I am not necessarily a worrier; I seem to find a way to make things work, find a way to enjoy what I got myself into somehow. But THIS! I have to say this is not what I expected. After 2 years, we had encountered 3 people who were unfriendly or downright rude to us/me. After 3 years, the number was still 3. Not 3 per day, what you can achieve in Germany or maybe even France. No, 3 people in 3 years being **NOT NICE.**

> In the last few weeks,

it seems like the Italian people that we run into have gone into overdrive. We only meet amazing people. On our trip, away from the cold and wet winter in Casentino, into the warm and more sunny areas of Italy, we passed by Napoli for a few nights. Napoli itself left us pretty much unimpressed. I felt it was somewhat dirty in many places, not super pretty - overwhelming - old - or stylish. But not bad either. We had the bad luck to spend 2 of our 3 days there under rainy and pretty cold conditions. BUT the Napolese people seem to be off the charts in terms of hospitality and friendliness. Our first encounter was being welcomed by the hostess of our hotel, Patrizia. I don't know that a person can be more bubbly, talkative, and nice. We were told before that the Napolitani are super hospitable people, and I think we received our first filling, right at the start. Picking us up from the internal car park, and while bringing us up to the hotel, she was talking about the historic hotel, how it's run, how she and her husband manage it, and how shopping is done, and ...... You get the idea. After the door of our room was finally closed from the inside of our room, we had to breathe in and breathe out a few times. Wheeeew, that was a little (she was so so short) thunderstorm, but so so warm and welcoming. Eventually, we made our way into the streets to get an idea of the city, went towards the sea, and had to find out where we ended up. There was no sea to be seen, as all space was used up by the harbor and tall buildings. On our way back up, we decided that another cafe was in order, and we literally stumbled into the next cafe on the street. Cafe coretto (Sambuca) and Cafe with pistachio cream, some sweets is what we asked for. What we got was our second "_welcome to Napoli_" of the day. A super welcoming, respectful, inquisitive barista and his helper kept us in the place for at least 1/2h 45min. We got to understand his family history, marital situation, places he lived in and with whom, how long, and why. We spoke about our lively travels through life as well and we had another "_welcome to Napoli_" behind us.   
  

> What is happening here?

Though we wanted to go and have a light dinner, we decided, enough is enough and we went to bed. Breakfast was served by Patrizia, and it came with many more tips and details about her life and her city. Incredible. How caring and communicative can someone be? We enjoyed the beautiful day and discovered parts of the city, then lunch was in order, and a little Trattoria came across our path. We were the last people permitted to have a delicious lunch that day at this restaurant. But the Boss almost exactly told us what to order, and why. You could hear he was not selling his stuff, he wanted to make sure we get the best his kitchen has TODAY. And it was too much and very good. The boss came during our lunch 2x to enquire if it was really to our liking and wanted to assure we had all we needed and also ate it right. Getting out of the place, even WITH paying, was a small but manageable communicative challenge. We decided to go up to the Vomero area, a wealthier part of Napoli, and get a cafe at a Specialty Coffee place. Ventimetriquadri is the name of the place, and I can only strongly recommend it to anyone who is passionate about cafes. I had my maybe 2nd-best and 3rd-best cup of coffee ever there. Vincenzo is a really great guy, an absolutely amazing Barrista and is the cause for these two cups of deliciousness. He seems to attract interesting clients (Rico), as we had the good fortune to meet during our 1st visit. Great conversations, not only on the surface but also going deeper. You could feel that Vincenzo runs this coffee shop for selling cafe, but also to meet people, and have people meet other people. What a joy of a tiny place he created, what a memory for us. We went back the next day, only to meet the same client (Rico) again and have another great cup. The rest of the day was really not worth mentioning, as it rained out of buckets and I was drenched on my little discovery tour on foot, just outside of Napoli. I had to run back to stay warm enough. No Napoli friendliness could make this afternoon reasonable.  
  

> Jumping to our time in Catania,

I must say that we arrived only about 1 week after a pretty devastating Hurricane came across the island and destroyed a number of areas really badly. Luckily for us, the Catania coastline was only affected in small areas. And super lucky for us, one of our favourite places, the harbor of Ornignia, was not at all affected. It is nicely sheltered from the waves and wind. Locals hang out there as soon as the sun arrives. With hangout, I mean, a bikini, swim trunks, a towel, and sometimes a guitar. To meet the people you just know about, and some of your friends from the area. To me/us as a newcomer, it seems like everyone knows each other and is super happy to see each other every time they see each other. There is so much laughter, so many smiles, kisses on the cheek for hello and then for see you later, so much talking going on. It is a joy just to see it. At least for me. Shape and age don't matter. Skin color differences just show how much you might have been in the sun this year already. And even we are welcome there by the second time we show up there with our little Brompton bikes. Then next time we get kissed hello and goodbye as well, and when we come again, what we do tomorrow........ This scene below is what drove me to write about Italian social behaviour and network, joy for life, and the need for friends to live. Not only live but live well. What is a life without friends for an Italian? I once got an answer to this question, during CORONA times. "if this goes on like this, social distancing and all, **_I will kill myself_**". Why live, when I can't be with my friends?
