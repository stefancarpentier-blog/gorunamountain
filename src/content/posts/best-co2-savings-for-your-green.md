I see cities, counties, states and the Feds struggling to make a somewhat rational choice in the field of reduction of greenhouse gasses. A long time ago in my Sustainability class at UC Berkeley, I think we came up with a pretty decent, rational and numbers based decision matrix.

Imagine yourself having to make the decision between two projects. Both cost $300,000

**One** would allow airplanes parked at the gate of San Jose airport to turn off their engines longer as the A/C needs to keep the plane cool during de-boarding, cleaning and onboarding would be run from the Airports electrical power, vs. the gas-guzzling turbines. Sounds like a pretty worthy project to support.

**Another** project would use the $300,000 to insulate and air seal residential homes for the winter, allowing the homeowners to save on their Natural gas bill and release less CO2 in the Atmosphere.

How do you come up with a decision which one is better? Simple solution I think. use a calculator how much tons of CO2 are removed per year for every $1000 spent and you have an intuitive and rational method to decide. In this case I made a calculation based on the numbers advertised by the respective organization and came up with this.

Airplane A/C substitute: 2 A/C units can be installed, 31,000 lb CO2 saved per unit ==> 62,000 lb CO2 saved

House insulation: 150 houses can be insulated, 1,100 lb CO2 saved per house ==> 165,000 lb CO2 saved

The house project wins. Is that too hard for an average Politician to understand?
