Today it snowed at our house. The first time it snowed here in the last 3 years. So naturally, we went for a walk to town. After not even 15-minute walking, my friend Natale called, and I went to see him at his mom's house for some coffee. Well, that was an Italian inviting around lunch time, for a coffee…. You know what that means. More. I get there, get to eat a full lunch, cooked by Paola, his mom, and it was Italian, great and amazing. Then I hoped for my promised coffee, but I was wrong again. Up comes her neighbour with a small bottle in her hand. Brown thick liquid inside. My friend Natale disappeared for a few minutes and came back with a pot of fresh snow, right from outside. What's going on? And while they all talk Italian really really fast, and I don't understand much, I keep hearing "Vino Cotto". I translated this to mulled wine or Glühwein. But I was so wrong. Then the snow was put into glasses, and the brown liquid poured on top. The brown liquid is cooked down from grape juice. Then you stir the mix, and like magic, it all transforms into a sorbet-like consistency. Then you spoon it up, and you get to ingest one of maybe the earliest sorbets of this world. The real earliest of this dessert seems to come from China and Persia.

Not only was this a very unique and a first time for me, but between Natale, an art historian and a sommelier for wine, and his mom, who has been living since 85+ years, an d both have never eaten this.
