I missed to mention yesterday that we managed to buy some amazing cheese from a sheep farmer along the way. A bit without electricity and I think 2 Rooms, 2 dogs. A gentile reminder of all the luxuries we take for granted.

Today we had to say good bye to Alec. What a joy to have him with us for 11 days. Really sad to see you go. We had another really really late start 9.30. or was already really warm again and we had to walk up a street for quiet some time. Which convinced us to take a chair lift up the first 400m climb. 1st time in my life taking the lift in the summer. We have very rocky and steep terrain here with lots of little lakes. There number if people became much much more. We are in the national park now and it shows. I did not go up the extra credit summit but used the time to sleep a little after my snoring infested night. We went along a few more beautiful lakes, then prepped for our big and hot ascent to a pass with filling water bottles and soaking all our clothing in water, just to cool us down there and before we go up. It was so worth it. Rosa in the lead we all cruised up the 700+m pass. Water bottles at the ready, legs moving strong. It took quiet a while till we finally reached the Pombie hut. We set up tent and jumped in the lake and meet or now"long" term friend Jasper from Belgium again. And interesting and nice guy who wants to bag some peaks for his license of a French mid mountain guide. We made dinner together sharing stories and cooking ingredients and meals. Only till the British man was called over to join us. Very interesting guy who seems to take his time with this Pyrenees crossing. Every day we hike, takes him roughly 3 days. Between yoga and meditation in the morning and the seemingly strickt time Limit of, 3h of hiking a day, his progress is slow. Super conversations that night and I'm the morning.

Day 13 I could convince the team to get up early to avoid most of the heat and get to our destination at a reasonable time. Breakfast at 6:30 and leaning around 8.00. The day started with a split team. Rosa and me, Ryan and Kim. Based on my experience 2 days ago, getting overheated, i wanted to minimize the chances of this, almost any way I can, knowing we will have a1350m ascent that day, in one stretch. It ended up to be out toughest day. The climbs was hard, but that was not the problem. Both groups managed to miss judge an intersection and avoid double checking the route, leading to us all clinking the wrong pass. We all fixed this in very similar ways, which lead is the the actual crux of the day. Climbing "Port du Lavedan" a series of hard to find crossings in Boulders, then Slate rock crossings followed by the challenge to find the right position to actually cross this pass, then crossing it in near vertical orientation, then not killing your team mate by intimidating any rockfall towards them. Not an easy task, for the mind and the body. It was followed up by a number of snow field crossings, in soft snow that where risky but not overly difficult. Seemingly endless lose rock descents taxes the already will utilized body and mind. I managed to slip on a flat section and rescued myself with a nice roll in the grass. No damage to me, but one of my hiking poles got crushed in the process. Even the mental break with some snacks could not avoid that fall and loss. We managed to do the 17km and 1350m climbing in about 9.5h. ~1.8,km/h. And we were moving almost all the time. Ryan and Kim managed to get here just as dinner was served. We had some very productive and honest discussions about group communication and how to refine it after dinner. The team of the Pombie Refugio was simply amazing. All women, only one young guy as the staff. One comes from the Pyrenees an cannot live anywhere else but I'm the mountains or on the Sea ⛵. She has lots and lots of energy, is funny friendly and really unique. Others where very personable and accommodating, nice..... Super team. Ryan decided that his shin split did not recover after our one ZERO day and decided to take a 3 day break from walking. We will meet him up in Garverin in 3 days. Day 14 (+1) We managed to get the team to breakfast at 6.30 with tent packed and backpack ready(almost). But it was still impossible to get started walking before 8.00 . The walk down from the hut was like in a fairy tale. Two mountains on both sides, the sun on the top, shade in the valley floor, a creek flowing through the few trees. 2h down hill and we dropped Ryan of with a friendly Spanish father and son to bring him or of the mountains. We started out 1350m climb on reasonably Smith trails, the Meadows sprinkles with rocks looked amazing in the rising sun. Several hours later we made it to the top and it was beauty again to be seen. We also met a Spanish family, shared good with them and good stories. We went down together, only to find out that the Mama is kind of obsessed with swimming in mountain lakes. So we joined in one doing session and the water was chilled down by the ice patches. Also small salmon was there. What a beautiful family. Activity, curiosity, honesty, togetherness; I loved to see all that. We ended up having dinner together in the Walton Refugio. Great times to be part off their group. Before I understood what was going on, they changed their hiking plan for the next day, just to be with us some more. We sleep outside again today after a cold shower. Day 15 (+1) Super sleep last night for me, Rosa has trouble as she forgot her pillow in the last Refugio. We had breakfast with our new friends and then headed out at a record time for us 7.45. It was plain out a little chilly and the morning light proved again to be amazing. It is so nice to walk when it is still cold. Pleasant conversations helped all of us to get over the continuous climbing, then, just below the first pass Marine was drawn to swim in another lake. Rosa followed the call while the rest of us tested, are and chatted away. The colored stripes on this one mountain gave it an amazing lol and structure. Beautiful! We made it to "Aulettes de Gaube" in unserer 5h with all the fun. Lunch there and beer for the group, great Spanish ham and a lot of love to go around. We exchanged phone numbers with a clear promise to see each other again very soon. Madrid is not so far and they have snow there at least once a year...... Good bye had to come and we headed for our destination of the day, Refugio de Bayssellance. In the afternoon sun we apply A/C water to all exposed parts and head up the 600m climb without much trouble. Meeting an Israeli gut who just started drawing this week, a Belgian guy and later after dinner at the hut meet his two German friends too. Living in the northern most corner of Germany, 5min from the Danish border. The views of the cirque de Gavarine were fantastic and we discovered a most displaced pyramid, right next to it. Another night in the tent this time higher and dryer. Good night. Going for internet tomorrow.... Finally after 5 days without.

Stefan
