## [HRP 2024 is coming – My biggest Party hike yet.](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/06/24/hrp-2024-is-coming-my-biggest-party-hike-yet/)

Posted on [June 24, 2024](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/06/24/hrp-2024-is-coming-my-biggest-party-hike-yet/)

We have now 3 people doing the full distance of the HRP (Haute Route Pyrenees). It is a hike of about 840km distance with about 50km of climbing over this distance. It traverses the complete length of the Pyrenees, trying to stay on the ridge for most of the distance. It traverses between the better known GR10 (France) and GR11 (Spain). These are long established Grande Routes, with trail markers and fully maintained trails. The HRP is not fully defined and multiple versions exist and are advertised. Some segments of the route will go over “use-trails” and are not really marked, which makes finding the right path to a summit or pass a challenge, as I read. But if it was too easy to do, it would not be quiet so interesting.

![](/photos/AD_4nXfXBHx1en2rdMcu-FdSX2o5tBA9K_EpT0Jas18QlLDhqHyv7HzGMHx83WzmCQlzbF2_qfvICbuOpAAvCc0eSDBYp3ScZUbmzd0b97IfUvzqp4MeYztC0W6TJJU99khaoIVIgzYN)

Rosa, Ryan and me will try to walk this trail from beginning to the end. Then we have this incredible group of people that will join us for parts of the way. 2 will start the trail with us (Christian, Alec) , Christian will leave us after 9 days and Kim will join us. Alec will leave on day 11, then Kim will leave us on day 18. On day 38 we plan to leave L’Hospitalet with 8 of our friends from California, now in Oregon and Canada. 2 Families, with 2 Kids each. After 5 days and nights together they will head back and we will go on for another 9 days to finish the trek at the Mediterranean Sea. I read a lot of trip reports about this HRP, but I never read about so many people doing it together to make this hike into such a party. I am super excited and a little nervous about it of course. But I really think we have a good team together and we are setup well for giving this our best shot and make it not only through this trail, but have a lot of fun in the process, see a lot of amazing things during this time.

I plan to send updates to this blog from the trail. Let’s see how the internet coverage will be along the way to make this happen as well.

### Some statistics of the team / trip.

- Youngest team member 11, oldest I think 63.

- Nationalities: USA, Germany, Japan, South Korea, Canada, France

- Base weight of packs: Between 6kg and 11kg

- Planned/actual departure 8th July 2024 from Saint Jean de Luz (not Hendaye)

- Planned finish date in Banyuls-sur-Mer 25 August 2024.  Actual finish day August 24.

- Actual rest days:
    - Larbet Refugio after 13 days of hiking.
    
    - Salardu on day 27
    
    - L'Hospitalet (Ax de Terme) on day 35 for 2 days

- Trail we followed was a mix, but mainly the Cycerone HRP guide. There are a few shortcomings to the way the guide is written in my view, but it is a very good guide through this concept of a hike, rather than a trail.

## [HRP Day 7](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/07/16/hrp-day-7/)

Posted on [July 16, 2024](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/07/16/hrp-day-7/)

We had a great night in this Gite’ etape in Iraty. We could cook there and eat outside, meet some new people. A great time. Alec and Ryan cooked some pasta and we had a bottle of beer and wine, we could stay outside till late. Very good evening.  
The hike started off with a super steep climb, right at the beginning, I felt strong and the views were far and fabulous again. But the wind was super strong. Because of this, we had to alter our route and decided not to climb the first 2000+m peak of our trip today. Lentil clouds on top convinced us to make this change. Right at the start of this traverse, I started feeling weaker, not bad, but maybe 75% of my usual. The stretch around these two peaks was harder work but again amazingly beautiful. After our lunch, strength came back a bit. Going down to our night camp Ardane. Sooooo many sheep today, incredible. We seem to be all set for a calm night today. Clouds roll in and out, it’s warm and we had our couscous coconut curry with veggies for dinner. Dates and other bars for dessert, tea to finish it all up. Another fabulous day.. It’s now one week since we started walking.  
19km 700m climbing.

## [HRP Day 10 Lescun to Arlet](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/07/18/hrp-day-10-lescun-to-arlet/)

THE SLIDE. Kim passed and was accepted in the group, of course. We walked 5km on tiny roads, but still steep uphill steep. The scenery changed distinctly to higher mountains and rocky. Very open and incredibly beautiful….. again. We climbed 1500m almost in one climb and it took a little bit of a toll on our legs. Even the traverse through this hugely open area was not only with simple footwork. I felt good today but was really tired in the end. A more basic Refugio waited for us, with sink washing but great people and a lake. Food was great and enough and we played two rounds of Mikado with a lot of fun. Tomorrow we want to move away from the main HRP trail and summit a mountain….. or not.  
Let’s see.

## [HRP Day](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/07/24/hrp-day-many/) 12-13-14-15-16

Posted on [July 24, 2024](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/07/24/hrp-day-many/)

I missed to mention yesterday that we managed to buy some amazing cheese from a sheep farmer along the way. A hut without electricity and I think 2 Rooms, 2 dogs. A gentle reminder of all the luxuries we take for granted.

Today we had to say goodbye to Alec. What a joy to have him with us for 11 days. Really sad to see you go. We had another really really late start at 9.30. It was already really warm again and we had to walk up a street for quite some time. Which convinced us to take a chair lift up the first 400m climb. 1st time in my life taking the lift in the summer. We have very rocky and steep terrain here with lots of little lakes. The number of people became much much more. We are in the national park now and it shows.  
I did not go up the extra credit summit but used the time to sleep a little after my snoring infested night. We went along a few more beautiful lakes, then prepped for our big and hot ascent to a pass with filling water bottles and soaking all our clothing in water. Just to cool us down at the start of the climb, before we go up. It was so worth it. Rosa in the lead we all cruised up the 700+m pass. Water bottles at the ready, legs moving strong. It took quite a while till we finally reached the Pombie hut. We set up a tent and jumped in the lake and met our now "long" term friend Jasper from Belgium again. An interesting and nice guy who wants to bag some peaks for his licence to become a French mid mountain guide. We made dinner together sharing stories and cooking ingredients and meals. Only till the British man was called over to join us. Very interesting guy who seems to take his time with this Pyrenees crossing. Every day we hike, takes him roughly 3 days. Between yoga and meditation in the morning and the seemingly strickt time Limit of, 3h of hiking a day, his progress is slow. Super conversations that night and around the morning breakfast table.

### Day 13

I could convince the team to get up early to avoid most of the heat and get to our destination at a reasonable time. We had another ~1300m climb in one piece to do and it deserved some respect. Breakfast at 6:30 and leaving around 8.00. The day started with a split team. Rosa and me, Ryan and Kim. Based on my experience 2 days ago, getting overheated, I wanted to minimise the chances of this, almost any way I can, knowing we will have a1350m ascent that day, in one stretch. It ended up to be our toughest day. The climbs were hard, but that was not the problem. Both groups managed to miss judge an intersection and avoid double checking the route, leading to us all taking the wrong pass. We all fixed this in similar ways, which led us to the actual crux of the day. Climbing "Port du Lavedan" a series of hard to find crossings in Boulder-fields, then Slate rock crossings followed by the challenge to find the right position to actually cross this pass, then crossing it in near vertical orientation, then not killing your teammate (Rosa) by initiating any rockfall towards them. Not an easy task, for the mind and the body. It was followed up by a number of snow field crossings, in soft snow that were risky but not overly difficult. Seemingly endless loose rock descents taxed the already well utilised body and mind. I managed to slip on a flat section and rescued myself with a nice roll in the grass. No damage to me, but one of my hiking poles got crushed in the process. Even the earlier break for the mind with some snacks could not avoid that fall and loss. We managed to do the 17km and 1350m climbing in about 9.5h. ~1.8,km/h. And we were moving almost all the time. Ryan and Kim managed to get here just as dinner was served. We had some very productive and honest discussions about group communication and how to refine it after dinner. The team of the Pombie Refugio was simply amazing. All women, only one young guy as the staff. One comes from the Pyrenees an cannot live anywhere else but in the mountains or on the Sea ⛵. She has lots and lots of energy, is funny, friendly and really unique. Others were very personable and accommodating, nice….. Super team. And they were French, can you believe it…..  
Ryan decided that his shin splint did not recover enough after our one ZERO day and decided to take a 3 day break from walking. We will meet him up in Garverin in 3 days.

### Day 15

We managed to get the team to breakfast at 6.30 with the tent packed and our backpacks ready(almost). But it was still impossible to get started walking before 8.00 . The walk down from the hut was like in a fairy tale. Two mountains on both sides, the sun on the top, shade in the valley floor, a creek flowing through the few trees. 2h down hill and we dropped Ryan of with a friendly spanish father and son to bring him off the mountains. We started our 1350m climb on reasonably smooth trails, the Meadows sprinkled with rocks looked amazing in the rising sun. Several hours later we made it to the top and it was beauty to be seen. We also met a Spanish family, shared food with them and good stories. It was the first time for me to have tasted Spanish Jamon. If you have not tried it yet. Do it. It is simply amazing. We went down together, only to find out that the Mama (Marine) is kind of obsessed with swimming in mountain lakes. So we joined in one session and the water was chilled down by the ice patches reaching into the lake. Also small salmon was there. What a beautiful family. Activity, curiosity, honesty, togetherness; I loved to see all that. We ended up having dinner together in the Walton Refugio. The staff there was not interested in us, unfriendly. I had to fight for us to get the dinner I reserved months ahead of time. Great times to be part of the Spanish family though. Before I understood what was going on, they changed their hiking plan for the next day, just to be with us some more. We slept outside again today after a cold shower.

### Day 16

Super sleep last night for me, Rosa had trouble as she forgot her pillow in the last Refugio. We had breakfast with our new friends and then headed out at a record time for us 7.45. It was plain out a little chilly and the morning light proved again to be amazing. It is so nice to walk when it is still cold. Pleasant conversations helped all of us to get over the continuous climbing, then, just below the first pass Marine was drawn to swim in another lake. Rosa followed the call while the rest of us rested, and chatted away. The coloured stripes on this one mountain gave it an amazing lookl and structure. Beautiful! We made it to "Aulettes de Gaube" in unserer 5h with all the fun. Lunch there and beer for the group, great Spanish ham again and a lot of love to go around. We exchanged phone numbers with a clear promise to see each other again very soon. Madrid is not so far and they have snow there at least once a year……  
Good bye had to come and we headed for our final destination of the day, Refugio de Bayssellance. In the afternoon sun we apply A/C water to all exposed parts and head up the 600m climb without much trouble. Meeting an Israeli guy who just started drawing this week, a Belgian guy and later after dinner at the hut we meet these two German friends too, living in the northernmost corner of Germany, 5min from the Danish border. The views of the cirque de Gavarnie were fantastic and we discovered a “most displaced” pyramid, right next to it.  
Another night in the tent this time higher and dryer. Good night. Going for internet tomorrow…. Finally after 5 days without.

### Day 17

We left Gavarine really late, but with one more team member. Gilad, an Israeli young man we met briefly and had great discussions with over dinner decided to join us for this segment 3. After I strongly encouraged him to lighten his pack by a few kilograms, he switched over with us to from the GR11 to the more technical HRP route. Just out from the military, he is fit enough to do all we can do and then some. Climbing out of the Gavarine valley, it was still cold, but great for climbing. I got a new set of hiking poles, aluminium ones and they are much much heavier than my previous ones. My shoulders complained about it the whole day. Nowadays, 1500m climbs in the morning don’t seem to be a big deal anymore. Days are a bit mixing together. I don’t remember details about this day.  
**Day 18**  
We start this day with a steep and long climb again. This time through the clouds. A part of the climb was on shingle type rocks, steep and exposed. The way up was a little tricky, the way down was plain out scary. It might also have been the steepest packed dirt descent I have ever done. I took my time. Two more climbs over adjacent passes and we finally saw the Baroude lakes. That day they presented themselves in cloud cover and with strong wind. It did not prevent us from a dip in the lake, drying off was fast. After dinner the wind died down almost completely and I could sleep under the stars, behind a big big rock. As the wind gusts kicked back into gear, I was a little more protected, at 2400m I was comfortable all night in my little sleeping bag. We met two Dutch people and chatted with them over dinner. It was really super interesting. Max and Natalie were hiking companions for a few days and now with us on our next day trip to Parzan for food supply and a cooked meal. We took an alternate route over a summit and into a scenic valley and hoped for a hitch hike down to Parzan for food supplies. Only 20 min later we were in a camper van on our way. Lunch in the heat of the valley together was great, in the shade. Then phone and battery charging, we did some shopping and we hiked in the baking sun along the road, only for 1.2km, but it was still no fun. The long long climb up the gravel road was much easier to handle with conversations with our new friends. Almost 4 hours later we arrived at a little reservoir, dipped and fetched a close campsite. Late dinner together and in the sleeping bag we go.

### Day 20

We got a good start, early and in a group. We picked up the two Germans along the trail, also just getting started. Super nice day in the morning but warm afternoon. The descent was through trees and not hard at all. We reached the small Refugio Viados at 12.30, before the lunch kitchen opened. After lunch we needed to face the heat on our way up 850m. First hot, then these last 550m up were super super steep. I felt strong and good. We are spending the night practically at the pass, near a little lake. We need to get out of the Rutt where we climb in the afternoon, somehow.  
Tomorrow is another day.

Day 21

Today we had a cold dinner with our far travelled salami and cheese.  
Looks like tomorrow might finally be an easier day. Let’s see.

We started our climbing from the unmanned Refugio reasonably early, parting from our newly found friend Jaime from Spain, a blueberry farmer with an amazing body and smile. The climb up the pass was pestered with big and loose rocks, same procedure on the way down to Lauset Refugio, a very modern hut. Spaghetti carbonara and a beer made us ready for the rest of the descent….. Almost. It got really steep and very very tiring as we descended for hours over big rocks with huge steps. Amazingly beautiful, but so hot and so draining. Getting to the road we hoped to get to the Refugio for some dinner and sleep. We got none of these and only received a sandwich. The only really unfriendly Spanish person so far made it a point to be sure we will remember him in a bad light. Sleeping in the forest nearby was fine and dark, but we could hear the reasonably busy highway in the valley.  
**Day 24**

We did not get started too early but made good progress as we finally climbed in the morning again. Phuuuu, finally. A good mix of fast trail and steep rocky climbs brought us up to the coll. The Refugio Restanca is on a beautiful lake, but you are not allowed to swim in it. But the warm hearted hut people and warm showers were well received. We enjoyed the first long afternoon without hiking in many many days. Welcome by the body and mind. Incredibly, our friend Max joined us in the hut after 19.00 . He is one tough guy making 1.5 of our least tough days in only one day…. Maybe more. What hormones do with you.

Large meadows and small forests in the middle of a big valley. That all ended at a parking lot and we ended up in a street. Before trying to Hitch the last street walk to town, we managed to get a delicious DIP in. We were in a good mindset and started walking. The resort looks big and interesting, having plenty of randonee ski options as well. Well well well, maybe one day. The hotel was easily found and our food supply packages have arrived there. Good good!!  
Clean sleep in a bed. Small things can make a difference.

## [HRP Section 4](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/08/11/hrp-section-4/)

Posted on [August 11, 2024](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/08/11/hrp-section-4/) •

### Day 26

Rest day in Salardu with a late wakeup, hotel style breakfast, visit to Vielha where we could buy new socks, gas, t-shirt and more as well as an Asian lunch with delicious beer and good view of town. Dinner with a larger team as Kilian and Kati could join us. Pizza, fresh salad and a little more beer. Great time to reunite as it was our last night with Gilad. He was a joy to hike with, but now he has a plane to catch from Zurich and hitchhiking there takes time. A final bottle of wine in an old place with some outdoor live music. One out of five songs were Spanish, the others were US hits from all different decades.

### Day 27

We took the bus from Salardu for the first 4-5km to the ski resort town, Bacquiera/Beret. Therefore our start, after the mandatory slide photos were taken. Not scenic but fast. Once out of the resort the scenery was great and we even got to chat with a group of Spanish friends/hikers. So pleasant. We arrived at the lake JUST as the clouds and fog started rising up and disappearing. What a show. We/I made a consequential route mistake and we ended up much too far to the right of the peak we were supposed to target. I should have listened to Rosa much more carefully. I will try to not make this mistake again and focus on the group dynamic much more during these decisions. We had to call off an attempt to rock climb our way up to the peak and carefully descended steeply instead on grass/pebbles down then 1/2h rock hopping to get to something easy and walkable. Short lunch and another 45min to an orange roof Refugio with a great lake. The indoor space filled up rapidly and we had 8 people sleeping outside. A cold night but we got an early start anyway. Great walking in the fresh temperatures. A long but pretty easy and scenic descent to our lunch spot refugio d’Alos with amazing Spanish omelette and Gazpacho. Super nice people and a cute little town. Maybe a bit late in the day but happy we started our 700m ascent in the heat. Beginning was super steep then more shallow in the shade. We finally have horses again. Even on our camp spot. So curious they almost ate our sleeping pad and Rosa’s socks drying on a tree branch. Relaxed afternoon with mountain scenery. ==> Happy. 😉

### Day 29

We got another early start today. Good as it gave us shadow for almost all our climbing. It was only 6 km to our next hut, but it is very rocky and very steep. Route Finding was involved and down climbing reasonably risky. While beautiful scenery was all around, with lakes and amazing rock shapes and colours, the going was very slow and tiring. 5h for 6km. At our little Refugio Pujol, we left or Backpacks and hiked up to a nice view point. The ridge for me, the summit for Rosa and Ryan. Amazing all around view, into France and into Spain. I could see way far into the plains of France. Grand view for sure, seeing the difference between Spanish and French Pyrenees. Because of the clouds, I will try again to sleep in the Refugio. So far, almost all the attempts ended with me escaping to the outside anyway. So let’s see.  
Day 30

The night was good and not too warm, none snored. We made our first departure without breakfast. It worked well. 1.5h into the walk we had breakfast next to a Cabana. A gentle descent through pleasant mixed tree forest. Loved it. Our lunch spot was combined with a lake dip, so so rejuvenating. The last climb to the pass went by quickly, Funk went to the summit, Rosa and I bailed on this and went straight to the hut for a beer and camp site search. Apparently the bunk beds in this (Certascan) hut are super tightly spaced. Food and staff were excellent and we had a good time talking to a group of Belgians. There was drinking too. Ryan spent some more time with them and had trouble finding the tent site…. But persistence prevailed.

### Day 31

A great night outside. Some rain, lightning and wind, but nothing serious. Apparently we might have to expect similar weather the next 2-3 days. Not bad having a little cool down. We started 7.30 in some clouds and also blue sky. The scenery was superb coming down and looking back at the hut. The reservoir spewed out enormous amounts of water and made noise in accordance with it. A nice little climb to the opposite side of the valley made for great visuals too. As we dove into the next valley, there was silence, all of a sudden. No sound from the water, just silence. The views were stunning towards the new lake and valley. What a beauty. Slightly downhill and pleasant. A tiny rock climbing passage to follow before we moved on to the new and bigger valley which demanded long whacky traverses over tall grasses and bushels, through trees. The only positive thing during all this traverse episode were the blueberries left and right on the trail. Tiny but so tasteful. A little blueberry taste explosion in our mouths. This must have been the lowlight of all traverses I have ever done. Not at all a pleasant walk. This was followed by a steep descent through the forest. In parts I felt like in Casentino Valley (my home) when I lost my path. After finishing 1000m descent we finally crossed the river and started our 1000m climb. Fortunately the trail was excellent and stunningly beautiful. We were treated to a lunch spot with a waterfall shower. What a super treat. The rest of the ascent was just as comfortable to manage. Just then we saw a young Spanish man walking down really really slowly, with two other men on his side. He had a bandage on his head and leg. They said they do not need any help, so we walked on with even more care and a humbled mind. Don’t forget to be careful with every step. We managed to be the first at the hut, only Max was already there, sleeping the whole morning. It is very very windy here, unusual….. Let’s see what the next days bring. Ooooh, super surprise. Kilian and Katie arrived at 20.30 at our little hut. They caught me peeing….. It is so nice to see these two again. It looks like the trail provides.  
**Day 32  
**We left without breakfast again. I like it a lot this way. The scenery in the morning was superb, the lake and the mountains around us. Then came the first group of kids, up the mountain. Probably 25-30 of them with I think teachers. Then another adult hiking group, then a women hiking group,…. Then then, in the end probably more than 50 people walked up to the lake this morning. We are so glad to have been there with only a handful of hikers. We stopped at the next refugio, 15 min out of the way. It was rewarding as they had a tiny bit of internet access. Slow, but the first we had in many days. A quick note to Muriel to say I am ok. Then we had to climb the daily ~1000m up. The large valley offered amazingly beautiful views. Then down the pass again traversing to a spectacular valley and huge block of a mountain. Very impressive. After a short walk on a gravel road we are on a trail again and move up the 300m quickly to our lake and camping spot. Cleaning, then dinner with amazing Spanish Jamon. 🍖. An Australian young guy asked if he could join us and we had our usual dish (Couscous, coconut milk, curry, vegetables and mushrooms), but with much entertainment. Good evening. Then, at almost 21.00 our two German friends came in. Super nice. The clouds provide an amazing spectacle moving in and around us here. Tomorrow should be a short day, but let’s see.

###   
Day 33

It was a reasonably short day and we enjoyed it mostly together. A different day for all of us. We climbed up steeply again in the morning before breakfast then descended to a ski resort. There we got our breakfast and had some more internet which was fast enough to call Muriel. Yepeeeeeeeee!! So good to hear her voice and hear what is going on at home and chat a little about what we are going through here. It helps my mind a lot. We took a gondola 🚠 down to a parking lot, then walked the rest of the way on an easy gravel road to El Serat. No shops here, just 3 or so Hotels/Restaurants. I ate 1.5 pizzas and a mixed salad. So so needed it. This was the start of my “Food intake program” to take care of my heart complaining about the lack of minerals and this and that. Then, to top it all off we took a bus a few km up the road to the start of our afternoon trek. We climbed over our pass and I finally figured out what helps me climb better again in some time. Stefan needs more FOOD. I will try to pack more and eat more from now on –> smile even more. We found a nice but pretty cold camp spot near a lake for all our cleaning needs. A social dinner with our German friends was awesome. I am learning about more plants that are useful for us in every day life. Thanks Katie!

###   
Day 34

Heading out early as we were high up. We had a nice breakfast at a tiny stream with amazing views. Traversing and climbing and descending as usual. We could spice it up with an amazing swim in an amazing lake with an amazing views next to Cabana Sorda. 5 Stars. We hiked to the next manned hut and called it a day. Well deserved. What a hut. Great atmosphere and great people around, just the breakfast was a little little.

###   
Day 35

  
It is the last day of this segment. A lot of descent over big boulders, first in the nice cold shade, then in the blazing sun. The technicallity of the hike finally eased off after the millions of  the boulders during 2-3h and we could actually walk, not climb only. After a short lunch with a Christian memorial nap, we descended into l’Hospitalet. A big let down from my point of view as it is so small and so unattractive, only one restaurant, one Gite’, and the most popular shop, the bakery, run by a Ukrainian Couple. It seems like these are the only people that understand, there is a daily stream of hikers coming that are hungry and thirsty and want to be fed. They are doing a great great job. Thank you. We had our group dinner here and met another Israeli lady, Rita. She has the warmest and most winning smile I have seen in a long long long time. She communicated little bo=ut in a way that really impressed me deeply. Sincere, sensitive, straight laced, and just plain winning. A see you later and it was finished.

## [HRP Party hike section](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/08/17/hrp-party-hike-section/)

Posted on [August 17, 2024](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/08/17/hrp-party-hike-section/)

After 2 zero hike days, away from l’Hospitalet and into Ax de Terme treated us well. Body and mind received some maintenance and relaxation, dearly needed. We picked up our two families from the train station and had a feast of togetherness and drinks, meals and conversations. I am so happy this all worked out. A little miracle.

### Day 38  

We got up late, for jet lag and other needs. Fromage Blanc was the first course, pastries from the patisserie second course. And off we went to the Refugio Besine. A really short walk with 700m climbing on mostly easy footing. We were in the clouds all the way up, some form of wetness was always around us.  
At the hut, card games and more rain. Reasonably cold, so we went inside for the rest of the time till dinner. So good to be with the friends we have not seen for many years.

### Day 39

Starting from the hut with a group of 11 is not a quick task. We started in some rain, climbing through rocks and wet grass. I decided to stick with the slow team, chatting with Mi-Suk and Kim. Things were easy till we reached the truly rocky parts. Visibility was near minimum 20-70m in the clouds. We made it to the hut with a few rays of sun during lunch, then 4 boys decided to climb an extra credit pass for some views and it did pay off. Very rewarding, but the way down was tricky and not so much fun for me. The Refugio room was high up in the building and I ended up sleeping in the dining room on the table again because it was too sticky and warm for me. Not a great night.

### Day 40

The day started like the last two…. In the clouds. The temperature is amazing for these foggy days, just the visibility is so limited. Very few sights worth sharing on days like this, but the hiking is so much easier when the temperature is reasonable. Yu managed to practically do the hike 2x with his 2h run up the two mountains. Incredible. We made it to the hut, the French host was friendly and motivated the food and beer excellent. I went to bed early today to catch up with the missed sleep yesterday. Sleeping outside of course.

### Day 41

Yu made it up the mountain one more time before breakfast.  
I slept like a log from 20.30 to 6.30. What a treat. Small breakfast and a late start of our two teams. I went with the turtle 🐢 team, the slow team of Thoma, Mi-Suk and Kim. What a treat of a walk it was on the short day. Small forests, wide Meadows, grand views, good conversations, dip in a gorgeous river. 10 of 10 today. And all that with dry feet. The Hill team came back a little tired of the steep down hill. Together we had a few beers and some food on the sunny and gently warm patio of a big restaurant. The food was excellent and we had good fun with the very sociable waitress. She spoke perfect english and we made it our group effort to ask her some questions about her life to guess where she really grew up. That was fun and she was fun. Sweet.

## [To the finish and Mediterranean sea](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/08/25/to-the-finish-and-mediterranean-sea/)

Posted on [August 25, 2024](https://gorunamountain.wordpress.com/2024/08/25/to-the-finish-and-mediterranean-sea/)

### Day 45

I am missing a bunch here, We ended up at a campground off the trail with two extremely nice, helpful and good cooking french people. They were amazing and it treated out mind very well.

### Day 46

A great morning in the campground with an 8.00 breakfast with pastry and baguette. Then a car ride (from out camp host)  to the next town, where we managed to get a taxi cab to the next starting point, skipping a whole day of the trail that we expected to be especially boring. We climbed a lot, starting only at 12.00 noon, in the heat, but found a lot of shade under truly magnificent trees. They kept changing and still stayed beautiful. We got to Illas with just enough time for a cold shower before dinner was served. Delicious dinner. The town had an unusual and weird touch. The night came in as probably the weirdest feeling night of the trip. Sounds, dogs hauling, strange atmosphere here. No need to go back there.

### Day 47

After a very weird and witchy might in Illas with dogs barking endlessly and chickens and roosters throughout the whole valley joining in a grand chorus several times throughout the night…. We all were ready to leave this place early. 7.10 departure. The day started already pretty warm but we could rest a few times for blackberry break. The scenery is not that great, not bad either though. It took us 4 hours to get down to the valley and freeway Le Perthus. Checking on dinner availability at the next Gite revealed that they are fully booked. This info together with the temperature forecast of 34C tomorrow brought us to a long long group discussion on what to do. Go on, or cut the chords and call it quit. We decided to move on with caution. Wait till 16.30 – 17.30 today to walk up the 800m to the finish point of the day, then wake up early and start walking in the dark tomorrow morning to possibly escape the heat or at least be able to manage it. Let’s see how all that will play out.  
  
**Day 48**  
The final day finally arrived. We got started hiking in the dark at 6.00. A new experience for this trip. It was already warm at this time of the day, and I was a little shocked and fearful of what was to come. Oven, inferno, sauna…. Who knows. The wind was super super strong,  and that seemed good at the time. Will be like a hair dryer in our faces in the afternoon? Who knows how bad the temperature will be. We made good progress and found the one spring that is still active along the way. Impressive trees along the way and another set of views of the so long desired Mediterranean sea. We all were pretty focused on time and temperature. Our breaks were regular but pretty short. It got really hot and steep towards the end and shaded areas rare. But im about 8 hours we managed to complete this monster of a hike and arrive at the Sea, diving in about one minute after arrival. What a treat, what a day, what a journey and experience.  
We had a nice leftover meal along the beach together then struggled to find a restaurant still servings good at 21.00 and had a night in a hotel, trying to process what just happened.
