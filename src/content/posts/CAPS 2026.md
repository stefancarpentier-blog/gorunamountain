  CAPS 2026

  After the **HRP 2024** , I kinda fell in love with the Pyrenees, more than already and over the next year decided another significant time, maybe another crossing through this beautiful mountain range was in order for me. Contemplating ideas and possible scenarios, I ended up with CAPS.   Coast to Coast Across the Pyrenees, Southern route. This time by bike.  As I could not find any premade routes that fill my bill of so many aspects (length, amount of climbing, beauty of the route, accommodation, northern vs. southern Pyrenees….. I had to come up with one myself. Not as easy as it sounds, plenty opportunities of small and big mistakes. In the end I think I managed to avoid many, still made a few of them. Finding the right team of capable as well as a little foolish was another, finding the time and managing all the rest of the details……

## DAY 4
Aug 18 2026
― Isaba 2 Yaca ---— Detour around the forest fire
  
  <iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=406169816&metricUnits=true&sampleGraph=true" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>


The morning started with an excellent Breakfast from a friendly spanish Baker lady, serving pain o chocolate, croissants and some cake. Truly excellent. But the 7 of us almost cleaned out her store,  several of the local clients in line walked away without their breakfast treat…. Happy but still sad. We continued a little bit down the beautiful valley and turned off to an even less populated street. Finally a pass that was not above 10% . My legs applauded. The way down we managed to lose our downhill rocketeer Tom as he raced past a crucial intersection. For him that meant to climbing back up and meet us in Argo in the region on Aragon (yes like in **Lord of the Rings**). We found a truly amazing deli which fuled is with pizza like flat bread and delicious sweets. Following this, with really heating up conditions, we ran a speed train down the valley towards Yaca. Managed to squeeze in a river dip, and Kim somehow was handed a bottle of water and energy drink by a complete stranger, nice and cold. People are amazing. The “last little stretch" to jaca proved more adventurous. The only real street to get us there was closed for fire danger, the Autobahn being out of question we made our way slowly through remote ranch style houses and gravel roads to Jaca. The campground told is initially, no no no FULL. **_NO MORE_**.  A few minutes and many words later she guided is to a small area. Not pretty, but doable. It was hot, we wanted to stop… I think it was clear to the camp host. The next visitors, asking for a camining spot were plain out rejected. So lucky for us. We chatted up a dutch guy on motorcycle and another one from Catalonia. Warm good people. Dinner and then a thunderstorm and rain that warranted putting up the tent again for sure. What a light show.

## DAY 5
AUG 20, 2026

Yaca 2 Broto 2 Waterfall – Wild camping

  <iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=406670369&metricUnits=true&sampleGraph=true" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

The first part of the ride was almost exclusively on main roads.  I wanted to stick as north as possible to the Pyrenees, so today we needed to take a bigger road to go east. As soon as we could, we took a gravel road instead, just a little further North. Some parts we doubted Komoots way to decide on what makes a ridable gravel road. One of the dry river crossings would definetely be impossible to cross with any water. Finally in the town of Gavin, we found an open Restaurant. This happened to be the 2nd of Spanish Restaurants that rejected us, clearly, not because of what was claimed, a lack of table space for us, but most likely because of our appearance. This brought down the "Hospitality reputaion" of the Spanish people we encountered on this and the last trip, from Impeckable to flawed in some spaces. Running on our well espablished "erica energy balls" or other reserves, we started pedaling up this canyon that got tighter with every kilometer we rode. Not steep at all, very pleasant to ride and nicely in the shade. We made it down to Broto, had some food, some drinks but decided that a few more km would benefit our quality of overnight camping. And tru it was as we ended up close to a little waterfall (Dip) on a big and wide meadow. The MOTHER OF THUNDERSTORMS came down to us that night. Yes, heavy rain, butthe lightshow and audio was truly intimidating. We felt very much safe in a valley and away from trees, but we wall were impressed.

## DAY 6
AUG 21, 2026

Waterfall 2 # Cañón de Añisclo 2 Pena Montanesa (HUGE Campground)

  <iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=406916329&metricUnits=true&sampleGraph=true" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>
  
This was supposed to be one of the most impressive days in terms of views. The National Park around the **Cañón de Añisclo** has a huge reputation. And, YES it is beautiful. But what I was not able to figure out was, the road leading throug it, is in fact a **ONE WAY ROAD**, of coures, not in the direction of our travel...… We climbed up the pass to get access to this area, saw all the cars parked along the way, stopt for pictures, saw the one way street signs, but non of us took them all that serious. Only till till till we got stopped by a National Park Ranger. In the nicest way he explained to us, we cannot go any further. The one way road goes on for another 12km and if the Police catches us, it will be expensive. If we get into an accident, if … if .… A long discussion between me and him broke loose, and I could not see any gap in his arguments. We had to climb back up, miss some of the views, and gain other views I guess. Not too hard, pretty long and it came down to a navigational error of mine. Not a proud moment of mine. I think I learned how to avoid these next time. Live and learn. Lunch at the top of the pass, down we go, looks to be taken pictures off. Our destinatioin needed to be the biggest camp ground I have ever been on. There where cabins, permanent caravans, mobile van camping, Tent camping. THey had 3 pools, gaming area, and picture that. They had their own water treatment plant. I am not kidding, there must have been thousands of peopl eon this "Camp Ground"  I would call it a "Camp City".

## DAY 8
AUG 23, 2026

Les Bordes (Buddha Camp) 2 Lavorsi
  <iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=407412587&metricUnits=true&sampleGraph=true" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>
  
  After our amazing camp experience, Bikke touring life hit us in the face and we needed to ride again. We headed up the "2 headed Pass" , Viu Llevata. Not so hard, but also playing with your mind a little. After a steep climb, you see the summit, and it reveals itself as only the 1st of 2 summits.  It's just a mountain, get over it. But the great news was, the second pass had a little lake, just at the top. It was probably the hottest climb we did  on the whole tour, and a dip, with all the clothing was in order. SOOOO good. The decent revealed amazingly beautiful vistas of mountains and vilages. What a treat. I must have taken the beauty point to drop my guard a little and managed to "work on my little Garminhandlebar device" while riding. → **Not good, Don't do that!** ← While I rode on a bike path, there where wooden posts in the middle of the cycle path, preventing cars to drive onto them. I managed to overlook one, hit my handlebar on it, performed a summersault over the handlebars and onto the ground. Small scrapes onthe knee, little scratch on the helmet, the bike had both break levers twisted in, the gear shifting cable was torn in 2 by the wooden post. I could shake it off, re-joined the group, found a Bike store, called and wen there (3km) to get it all repaired in 15min. How lucky was that!!!! The rest of the group went on to our (now earlier) Campground and I met them there with only little delay. A littel camp ground with a little town right on the other side of the river.   This was projected to be, and worked out to be the toughest day of the trip. 1500m climbing in one go, ½ of it on sealed road, ½ of it on rough gravel. So rough, so slow, so so so.… Well, the beginning 10km were to warm up an dto catch up with what my planned route stated. The expectation of a bakerydid not materialize, but a cafe and a supermarket did. Brazed, fuled and prepared we went for it and it prooved to be a steep one, but manageable, a beauty of a pass to be sure. Small canyon, tiny street, steep canyon walls, almost no cars. The guest house came up JUST at the right time. We got COKE, chips, goat joghurt, some cafe.… and all with the biggest smle. What a treat of a rest stop.
