We moved to Tuscany, Italy in May 2020. It is a move I will find hard to forget. It was a move that we thought about long and hard. It is a move that I am still super excited about and am happy and a little proud we managed to pull of. So so many reasons were and are good, to make a change like this.  
I am happy we did come here. Here is not only Europe, not only Italy, not only Tuscany. Here is The Casentino Valley. It was also important for us to escape from the USA, from CA, and the Bay Area. The grass is always a little bit greener in an area you don't know quite so well yet. You don't know some of the little things that become harder over time. But I have yet to find any of the HARD things here in Casentino. It is almost 3 years now that we live here.

  
People are truly kind here. There is a great mixture of simplicity here, without letting life out of sight. I am so happy to be able to stay in our home for a day or 3 and not need to go into town. When I do, people know me, at least some do already. When I want to go into nature, it's truly right outside our door. I can hike to knee-deep snow in 1 1/2 hours. I skied on our property, can get water from the property, and planted tons of fruit trees with the hope to get a lot of our fruit in the summer from there. I am building Vegetable beds, from the outside (round) part of trees, getting "horse shit" from a neighbor, compost from another place, and Lava sand from 15km away. All that is down in a team effort. A Carpenter, a Blacksmith, and me. Why, because we three want the same thing and help each other out, doing something together. That is all stuff I can do, can do with others while getting to know them.

  
There is one thing though. One I did not take lightly but did not think would be as big of a problem for me as it now is. It's to learn this Italian language. The last time I started learning a language was when I was 10 years old. Barely more than 20-30 years ago, or was that even more….. I am making progress, finally. But it took a lot out of me. Not only that I am German and Italian is miles away from that. Italian is a roman language and is a "high context" language. One word can have several meanings, depending on the context it is used in. OK, I say. Give me the rules and I learn. What I had to find out the hard and long way is, learning a language is much more about remembering things than about figuring things out, and deriving things. This is not engineering at all. It is something, I had to find out, something I am not very good at. I was not used to that and it was (and is) a battle tonight with yourself. Setting your own expectations and NOT fulfilling them. Took me a long time to slow down to the pace I was able to learn. Not a fun process and I came out a bit bruised. What did not help was the fact that Italian is pretty close to French. And Muriel proved to have a memory, which I am still scrambling to find, up in my head. Sooo, Muriel progressed just fine, more than fine. And I did not progress in any similar way. It was not because of a lack of trying, I bought 1/2 meter of different books and tried to find a way to get some Italian into my head. Took some online classes and had to sign off after the 2nd or 3rd session as they were all FLYING past me, with my head spinning. I tried a local so-called teacher. He seems to work for others but we did not get together very often. I am finally on a path to "piano-piano" moving my head into this really complex language. I trust myself now to start a simple conversation with random people, I still don't understand close to 100%, but I can speak, it is rarely correct, but the kind (gentile) Italians react so well and keep me in my comfort zone so I keep talking about stuff I can. PROUD with the need for more PROGRESS.  
One more issue seems to be a constant here in Italy. It is complexity. (Almost) Nothing is really easy here. I am amazed at how convoluted almost all bureaucracies are here. We started a building project, submitted plans on how we wanted to do things, follow the plan EXACTLY, then submit the finished plan to complete the work in the cadaster system. It gets rejected. WHAT?????? I ask to do something, the agency approves, then said agency rejects the exact implementation of the plan we submitted. I wanted to go ballistic but was told to hold off on that. The legal mills worked away, phone calls are made, emails were sent, discussions are had, some plan footnote had to be changed, then the whole work was accepted. Only a mere 6 weeks after it was rejected. End good, all good. But WHY????? Tell me why it has to be this chaos, tumult, and a legal vacuum created, just to fill it with …….. You get the picture.

  
\==> Italian is hard  
\==> Italian bureaucracies are complicated  

But if this is the "price" I have to pay to get to live in this beautiful country, so it is.  
California spoiled us so so much with weather and temperatures, I can no longer accept having 4 months of cold (California-compared-cold) and 2 months of snow likely. This is the 3rd winter we are here. Yes, I know, it's already almost 3 years here. And I think we have to find a warmer place (in Sicily) so we do not have to hibernate. I will need to restrict my Skiing, as the amount of snow falling in the southern alps is so pitiful and I cannot handle the resort-type skiing anymore. Maybe a few BC tours in the Apuan Alps, or in the western Apennine to calm the craving.
