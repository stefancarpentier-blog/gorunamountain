**Aug 13th at 7.15** I left Cassandre’s house in London and started my Bike trip to Nuernberg.

I used Komoot to plan the trip and to help me navigate.

Getting out of London was just as I expected. Gladly the commute traffic mostly went the other way, but the street maze was quite amazing. Every 30sec I get another direction thrown at me from the Komoot navigator. That together with left side traffic and potholes, bike lanes, cars, and pedestrians, I was working hard to crash into anyone and not run over a high curb, or run into an oncoming car. In short, my small town brain was working overtime for 2-3 hours to get through this. The 80 or so kilometers out of London are not really worth it. But I felt like riding them, just to see if my expectations are true or not. No real point to ride London Rochester. I would take the train next time (~60-80km).

Rochester seems to have a good amount of history and respective buildings. I did not stop there at all though.

The area around Canterbury is nice and the stretch from there to Dover is well worth the ride. Small roads and even Footpaths were my ways to get through there. Really charming, hilly and Rural. All Rural!! with all the smells and dirt coming with it. Where I had lunch, I had to find out that a Cup looking like a Yoghurt, a Strawberry painted on the side does not have to be a Strawberry at all. I ended up drinking Buttermilk mixed with vegetable poi as a whipping cream replacement…. Nutritious it was, not very well tasting though. I had the impression Komoot really selected great paths where it could. I got to ride total dirt roads as well as single track on the way to Dover. I was quite surprised in a positive way. The clouds gave way to drizzle, gave way to rain gave way to a downright opening of the sky just before I got to Dover. What a mess. Dover Freight terminal is not for the timid. Huge trucks and lots of them, no clear signs and no-one really knows what to do. Of course, I managed to get on the ferry, met a fellow Cyclist that did a similar route than me, he went on to ride another 120km after getting to Calais, all the way to Bruges, my destination for tomorrow. What a STUD.

At the second Hotel, I found a room for the night. France is on vacation. Will see how this plays out in the following days.

Dinner at a good Brasserie with Creme Brûlée to finish it all up. Nice.

\

\

**Aug 14th**  Getting out of Calais and on the road along the coast to Brugge. Can you spell FLAT? I had to find out that it seems like the complete coastline is built up with Hotels and resorts. I made a detour to see the coast and had to find out it was a royal waste of time. Ugly and crowded.THe roads and towns vastly improved in beauty. The cycle path mostly went along canals and if not still was a set of small lonley roads. I managed to "get a ride" with a bunch of Road riders and was propelled into a 35-40km/h with little or no extra effort. NICE. THe People of Belgium seem to be very social and communicative kind. Two or three nice interactions convinced me. Then the flats began to come my way. 1st a 2cm nail, then a glass, then something else. I made it to Brugge with a few last pump strokes, got new tire and tube and got a hotel room in spite of a bank holiday coming up.... Lucky me.

Brugge is a really nice little town, LOTS of tourists and some really nice canals and buildings. It was worth an afternoon of strolling.
