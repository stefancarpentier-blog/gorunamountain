I am super excited today. I had a day of bread yesterday and I think it paid off. I made 6 loaves of wonderful bread, all with slightly different recipes or methods to try and learn from the differences, what causes what. I started feeding the starters the night before for two, made the Leaven for one. Three bread types were the goal:

1. Rye starter, yeast addition, my usual method
2. Rye starter, yeast addition, dissolving the starter in the warm water, then adding the rest of the ingredients
3. Rye starter - converted to a wheat starter the night before, dissolving the starter in warm water like in 2) then following the exact description from the Book "Tartine" in terms of finishing/forming the loaf before baking (this is not like me usually!!).

I was so so surprised how different the loaves of bread came out.

The absolute surprise was that my original method 1) worked out the WORST, still good, but a royal pain to work with. The dough was super soft and runny, did not hold any shape and the final bread did not have a lot of taste itself. What I learned was that the time I gave the dough in the Bulk rise made a huge difference in how the dough consistency came out. for about 25-35min after mixing the ingredients, the dough needs to sit and rest, I think the starch and Proteins seem to soak up some of the water and help hold the dough in shape after that. I did that pretty well on the 2) version, as well as I, did use the warm water for the final dough to dissolve the Leaven. It seems to spread out the leaven rising power better throughout the dough. ALWAYS do it, it's easier too.....

3) became the winner (second from the left) as I followed the gentle folding techniques from the book Tartine to help the dough in it's ability to "spring rise" in the 1st minutes in the oven. It seems like the folding and increase in surface tension, without adding any more flour to the dough, "spring-loads" the dough, and the entry in the hot oven triggers the release of the spring loading. This is my 1st sourdough only (no industrial yeast, bread that rose to this level of perfection. I am so amazed. Now, there is still one more thing I learned. I added one more loaf directly in the oven, not inside a dutch oven. THe difference was just as amazing. The leftmost loaf is the one I did without the Dutch Oven. It resulted in a much more flat loaf ==> the lack of moisture and lack of intense radiant heat in the first minutes did not allow the loaf to rise like the other. ==> Duch Oven ONLY from now on.![DSC02203](/photos/dsc02203.jpg)
