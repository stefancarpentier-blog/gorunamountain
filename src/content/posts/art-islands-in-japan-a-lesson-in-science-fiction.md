We heard a lot about the ...shima Islands in Japan, thank you, Cassandre, for priming us with this info. We decided we will finish off Japan with visiting them for two days. What a great decision that was.

Naoshima, Teshima, Inujima, and Megijima. All are located close to each other and located between Okayama and Takamatsu is the Osaka Bay. There are long stories on why, how, where and who made all that ART happen on these islands. You can read up on all that. Pretty interesting. The result, for us as travelers are installations in odd places, different settings and vastly different in depth and meaning. The art museum on Teshima is by far my favorite. The most unique and impressive structure I have ever been to. Impressive on a number of levels. _**I want to give a spoiler alert**_. If you plan to go there sometime soon, please skip the next section.

From the outside, this thing looks impressive. A white and flat elliptical blob made from concrete. Reminded me of a drop off water, just on its side. It's got two giant holes in it, right on the top. That's all you see from the outside. Scifi. Ok. What's going on inside? It takes a ticket and walk and a speech and your shoes off till you can find out. It comes down to, not much else. One giant flat surface, concrete, and the sun 🌞 coming in through the two big holes. People are sitting around on the floor in no order. Really really cool and impressive and touching and space ship like. But again close to and in the middle of nature at the same time. This giant concrete blob felt human at the same time. Two thin strings hang across the holes. With no wind, they seem to resemble a drop style shape as well. Hmmm. Then I was told to watch where I step on that flat concrete floor. There are art installations in there I hear. Hmmm, where are they, I ask myself. Then I see something moving. Drops of water on the ground are moving sideways, combining with others, then stopping again. Others join in and there is movement in the drop again. Then it gets bigger, accelerates and forms a snake-like shape, growing thicker then thinner, then running into a bigger puddle. You see them joining and see the waves this event joining event causes in the puddle. I am completely fascinated and taken in by little drops of water. What the hell is going on here? Next, I see what looks like a golf ball on the ground. Once in a while, tiny drops of water escape it and join the water game, but with much faster horizontal speeds than the others. Tiny streams are created and then they disappear. They disappear into tiny holes in this concrete floor that looked 100% flat to me 7 minutes ago.

![](/photos/artist6065183727613553091.jpg)

I detect more and more minuscule gradients on the floor. I see the source of the water. Little holes in the concrete are emitting water at different rates and intervals in different ways. It is all a huge water playground with almost no water at all. You look back up and see the glaring bright Sun casting its shadow on the ground and I am trying to take in this as well. Then the is the septation with nature again and still being so close and present. In the meantime, none says a word. There are 20-30 people in this structure and it's almost completely quiet. Only in Japan! This is amazing. I am fascinated.

If I have not made it Cristal clear yet. This thing is amazing and I will never ever forget it. Mumu loved it so much, she said she cried in the middle of the experience, among all the people, it was so beautiful. It was a lot to take in.

Thank you, Rei Naito and Ryue Nishizawa, artist and architect of this impressive structure and piece of art.

I think it is best to just rent a bike and tire from one at installation to the next. Teshima island has actual hills, so if you are tall like me I recommend the e-bike option. I felt like on a kiss bike, it was so small. There are free shuttle buses as well. None of them are far apart and the views are great with good weather. Teshima seems to have smaller and more private feeling sites whereas Naoshima has some heavy hitters in the mix as well. Do not miss the [Chichu at museum](http://benesse-artsite.jp/en/art/chichu.html). They are two really amazing rooms you don't want to miss.

To top it all off, we managed to meet a lovely NZ artist lady in line to the ferry. She seems to use bees to install social change and is involved or leading a whole lot of interesting projects. I am looking forward to hooking up with her to learn more about it.

Mumu and I have a quick dinner before we head back to Okayama by train then I slip into my little sleeping compartment. So small and unusual for me. Good thing I brought ear plugs. It was a valuable experience to have, I am in no rush to do it again. I had more space than when camping, has power outlets, TV.

**Shinkansen** is pretty awesome. Yes, it's a Tain. But it clearly takes a huge amount of little things to be in place, every day to get all these trains to be there, clean and on time, every time. And if you miss one, like us, we waited 9 minutes till the next one came. As a comparison. Trying to take a "train" from San Jose to San Francisco you will be hard pressed to find two trains leaving for the same destination in less than 20-30 minutes intervals. Usually, we have 1-hour intervals. Caltrain takes 1 hour for 76km. It's noisy, everything vibrates, it smells, it's dirty.

Okayama to Osaka took 50 minutes for a distance of 179km. We left the station on time to the minute, smooth, quiet, food service provided.

In my view it is an insult to use the same name for these two connections. They feel worlds apart.
