So this is our 2nd time in Berlin. Last time we were there in the dark of winter solstice. Dark from 4.30pm to 9.30am. This time we had some serious heat wave(s) and we had light from 5.00am to 10.00pm. Berlin is really unique. I don't think I have ever been to a city where it seems like everyone speaks at least one foreign language. And in Berlin, I felt this way in almost all the places we have been. It's the capital of Germany and the employees of a supermarket or Coffee shop speak English among each other and then to me. No big deal. I think that is pretty amazing. And comparing it with London, it did not feel crowded at all in Berlin. Anyone home? The public transport system, well, it's great and it is big and stuff works. I love it.

**Wannsee**, a sizable lake in the south-west of the city. I have heard about it since I was small. My aunt talked about it and the Grunewald, I heard this super fun and upbeat [song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H63kiYgMUEM) from the little girl that goes there in the summer time... I had to finally go there to check it out. Accessible easily through train and tram you walk for 10 minutes and you are at the beach. A pretty big beach and the structures there for the client's needs are massive. The water warm and I can see how local Kids are completely excited to go there as a summer getaway. And the water was so warm. Then there was the "Currywurst and Beer" on the way out. German - Beer ok, we all know that. But Berlin puts a different spin on this Beer. Berliner Weisse. It's the one drink that spells BERLIN. It's "obergaeriges bier" and syrup is added to it in the glass. And then, here comes the killer. You traditionally drink it with a straw. I added currywurst to it with fries to make it a full meal here in Berlin. I was super happy to have it but will not need to do this again tomorrow. I am from Nuernberg, not Berlin. ![Weisse](/photos/weisse.png)

### THE WALL

It is such an impressive time and event for me. Germany got divided for this Socialist experiment. 3 nation leaders decide how to divide the EU world at the  [Potsdam Konferenz](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdamer_Konferenz), August 1945. One of the results is this giant wall, going through the middle of a city. As it was finally implemented in 1961, the scenes of human desperation were pretty shocking to me again. People jump fences as they were built, families jump out of windows to be caught by firefighters with large fabrics to ease their landing, tunnels are dug and even East soldiers are deserting to the West. The impressive thing today is how it is omnipresent in Berlin. It is sure that this is intended to be made unforgettable and undeniable.

We got pretty lucky with the weather. I think a little too lucky. 2 days of about 40C in our 6 days in Berlin. Phew. The 1st day we managed to get to the Freibad in Kreuzberg. It was crowded, the line to get in was 30 meters long and seemed to only crawl.

Inside I think I only saw 10% Germans. The whole place seemed to be taken over by the Turkish people. I don't mind them at all but was surprised how few Germans were there. Boy, it was hot, there was every reason to escape to a waterhole. I saw fully covered women swimming and splashing next to their husband in just swim trunks. In my view, the women seem to get the short end of that societal stick. All the work and no real fun and freedom of expression. -- Forget about swimming in this pool. No way! It was so full, incredible.
