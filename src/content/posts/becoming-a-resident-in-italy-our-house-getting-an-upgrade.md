### House and grounds

After arriving in Casentino, Quarantine, and an awesome welcome by our host family, followed by House cleaning and making the immediate yard operable we could get to the place where the actual work of getting our new home in the shape we wanted could begin. 3 weeks after we signed and bought the house, the construction workers moved it and we can now say we have more of a swiss cheese of a house that we ever thought. Decisions are made every day, tiles selected, floor coverings selected, radiator locations selected, sizes and shapes selected, fireplaces removed and and and. Application for our pool is submitted for a 4 months processing time. We found ways to open all the gates on the hillside of the property so we could finally get access to all parts and see what we actually bought. This sounds silly and maybe pretentious. It's not. Just to be able to make it into one part, I had to work on clearing the gate from Blueberry bushes for 2 hours, then cut the metal chain with my hand saw, then use the hulk of a mower to cut a trail in the 1.5 meter high grasses, just to get closer to the area of the 40 walnut and 7 hazelnut trees we bought but never saw. Finding the way in was not trivial, cutting it and making it accessible for the regular person was another 4 hours of cutting grasses and whacking bushes and trees. Now we could SEE the trees, we still could not walk there easy. The light into this orchard was truly magical, soft, and fresh in the morning. I loved it. So, I got to work to get the area cleaned out and made accessible. Blackberries gone wild, extra trees growing everywhere, Wasps in the ground. 4 days of mowing and I was through. Then some more cleaning of the dead branches and this area looked somewhat like an orchard. Next in line is the second access gate tot he area, 1,5h of mowing and cutting the gate clear of the vines and and and the gate was open. Then the future vegetable field needed a second clearing, making it ready for tilling and planting next year. We still have an area on the bottom of the house, it is so steep and overgrown and speckled with rocks, no regular machine could clear it, not even close. So Mumu hired a mini excavator with a DINGBAT at the end of it. In a little over a day, all was whacked down and we could at least see what we have. Then we had the pleasure of uprooting all the little blackberries, or they would come back in a few months, similar to what they were like as we started. We got some help from our friends Medi and Marko, but then we were left alone with it. We now need to find a willing and able helper again with all this. We are not 21 years old anymore and the body shows signs of limits. This is really wearing on us. One morning, Mumu could not even lift up her cup of tea in the morning. Too much.

I ventured in the lower part of the property the 1st time a few days ago. One gate opened, and then making my way down-hill for 2 hours, cutting and whacking and getting a little lost and finding my way again.... I still could not make it to the end of the property.![Macia-2-street-path](/photos/macia-2-street-path.png)

### Residency

Meanwhile, we did a lot and a lot of work to gain residency in Italy. It is not a simple thing to do and you need it for pretty much everything. Just to be able to buy a car and insure it, residency is required. And I have to say, the Italians came for the rescue. The people are so overwhelmingly helpful and kind as well as understanding. We went to the city of Pratovecchio-Stia commune and wanted to find out what we need to bring. the first kind lady told us as we live in the rea of Porciano, we needed to go to the Stia office of the commune. She said it in the nicest way and even described to us in detail how to find it. It's not that hard. Turn left on the main street, drive to the beautiful grand villa on the left, park and you are there. But you could see she cared for us to make it there. And this was still in the pretty crazy COVID times. You follow the rules of distance, mask, and polite behavior ==> you get all the politeness and help you want. At the new location we find out which documents we will need, we get them printed and come again the next day. Well well well, stupid Stefan. You need these in the Italian language, not in German and/or English. Well well well. This is where the action started. Getting them translated and certified - sworn - and stamped was a different matter. The Tribunale needed to be involved, they are open only 2 days in the month, due to COVID. What do we need to bring, who translates them, who can swear for their accuracy.... so so many questions and few answers. We managed to get a temporary and limited residency document so I could buy my little dream car. Fiat Panda 4x4 at the tender age of 31 years old. Super excited I got it and got it insured after some long search and little find. The solution is of course the Italian/communicative/face2face approach. As soon as I went into the office of an insurance agent, explained my situation, I found a very willing and motivated lady on the other side of the counter that found out I could register my green beauty as a historic vehicle and could get it insured for E170 per year. This is a big deal as others (like Mumu) have to pay almost 10X that as we don't have yet shown our driving record inside Italy.  Soon Mumu had her Panda too. A much younger version, only 4 years old. Equipped with that we found a translator for our documents and 1 months after our start in the office of the Stia commune, we have a valid application for Residency in Italy. We should receive our ID cards next week. Phffffff Another milestone, one more off the shoulders.

Oh, not to forget and underappreciated. We had our first visitors.
