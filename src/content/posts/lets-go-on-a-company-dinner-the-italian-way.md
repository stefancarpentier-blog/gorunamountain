As many of you know, I am in the finishing stretch of remodeling our new home in Tuscany, Italy. People are passionate about their work here, diligent about doing the job right, on time and and and. Many of the stigma points I had of the Italian way to live, to be, to interact, to work proved to be not correct at all and that was a good thing. What might have helped is the fact that we moved to the Tuscany area. By many people here, it is considered the Switzerland of Italy. All I can say, what we were able to accomplish in 1 1/2 years is pretty amazing; I doubt I could have done that in Germany, I really do.

Now, I am a little off-topic I think. Last week, the team of the construction company wanted to get together for a dinner. NICE I thought. Then it became clear that I will be invited too. As I am such a big customer of theirs, they wanted to all see me as well. Speaking was not all that possible as I still only speak a very very small number of Italian words and it is pitiful to hear the stuff that comes out of my mouth that way. Nevertheless, they invited me. NICE move I thought. I was directed to the local watering hole, pizza place, and all together community hangout. The fact that they brew their own beer on the 1st floor, the restaurant is on the ground floor helps to make this a loved go-to place. Prices are reasonable, food is really good and plenty, service is good, people are friendly. Good place for a bigger get-together like they planned. 9 people in the company, 8 could make it.

When I arrive, I see some known faces, some new ones that do work behind the curtain for customers. I slowly figure out the mix of people. Some Italians, local from the Casentino Valley, some Albanians where I was quickly advised of the differences between northern and southern Albanian people. Then a few people from Romania and then me. Lots and lots of food was brought up to the table. People actually do physical work here, and you can see that they energize accordingly. The talking was plentiful, loud, and with plenty of passion. But what is never forgotten is the Italian politeness and respect for each other. While opinions are voiced without a hand in front of one's mouth; loud and clear, it is always done politely and in style. While I really could not understand most of what was said, it gave me the opportunity to look at the faces and non-verbal communication that went on. Fascinating. People liked each other and respected each other.

The boss of the company sat there, talked, and listened just like any of the rest. He was never treated in any way differently than anyone else. The respect he received, was given to everyone. Now here comes the deal. They sat together as a set of friends, for 3 hours, really having a good time seeing each other and talking it up. When it was time to leave, I mean, pay 1st, then leave, It was made clear to me, that I will not pay. Hmmm, OK. The good customer does not pay. I get it. Then I find out, not only does the boss not pay for all of us. No! He does not pay at all. The complete staff invites him (and me) for this dinner. The reason that was given to me was; he invited everyone last time, it was time to pay back. **SO**, not only did pretty much everyone come to the company event, they had a good time did not have to get bribed by a company T-Shirt and logo water bottle. Then to top it all off they each paid for the dinner for themself and for their boss.

\==> Italians seem to love life with others, they seem to need their friends around and are not shy to make new acquaintances quickly. To me, they are truly passionate people who do to want to disappoint. They are inclusive and very polite. Etiquette is a thing many know and act in accordance with. In an interesting twist, it seems to me that Italians are true individualists at heart, but do not want to miss the strong and tightly woven mesh of a social support network. There are no libertarians here that I know, only in the region of Livorno I have seen true socialists and even anarchists. There are not many extremes here, people stay more in the middle. I am so happy we moved here. Imagine, one day I will be able to speak their language and talk maybe 1/2 the speed they can. I would be up the moon and back.
