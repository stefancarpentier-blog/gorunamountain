### The forrest here is simply breath taking.

Two Gaigin on Kyushu

We rented a van and went on a drive to the South-West side of this green island. It literally felt like diving into an ocean of different greens and different textures, all was in the shape of a forest but felt like an ocean. I will post some pictures, but like sometimes, they don’t do justice at all. The tree-canopy bulges out like mushrooms or big heads of a Broccoli. They bulge out of other shades of green and other heads of Broccoli or spiky pine tree shapes. Then you imagine all this planted on a curvy hillside, shallow and then steep, valleys and ridges and all of it going on for many many kilometers. My mind was thoroughly blown away.

### Chiran Peace museum

We managed to get to the Peace Museum (known as 神風 - Kamikaze - Tokko mission). In the city of Chiran a unique airport and training facility was built at the end of WW2. Training young soldiers to fly deadly missions with small planes, loaded with lots of explosives and run them into high-value targets. A total of just over 1000 of these missions were flown, 40% from Chiran, the rest out of Taiwan. I think all were done with the target to prevent the occupation of Okinawa by the US forces (an important strategic point for the US to stage attacks into Tokyo area). It is tough to see how all these lives were sacrificed, for the good of a country, to defend against another country's aggression, … There are so many reasons that are used to justify any of these actions. I am just sad to see the constant need for this type of waste of human life. It makes me terribly sad.

### Eating

Japan has some delicious food, excellent food in fact. New tastes, textures, and experiences here are really unique and very welcome. But Mumu and I found that the average food presented to us here was just ok. The other challenge with going far south on Kyushi was that we were almost the only non-japanese people here. Very few are prepared for people that don't speak or read their language. Ordering at a simple Ramen shop consisted of merely saying YES, to all questions and then ordering one big and one small bowl of whatever we just ordered. Interesting, but it worked out to be great Ramen and a great rural experience with the ramen delivery man helping to prepare the Ramen across the counter from Grandma who runs the show in the shop. Hmmmm. Then, that same day we went for a sports-health food restaurant with pictures on the menu. What could go wrong here? You see what you order. All parts came out tasting interesting and enjoyable, except one. Fermented Soybeans (Nato?) and I was the lucky one to get them. As I pull the first part of it from the plate, I see long spiderweb-like strings forming. Hmmmm. Then I dared eating it. And I now hope I will never have to swallow anything like this ever again. I know it is a Love it or Hate it type of food. I don't like it at all!

## Sand bath and Onsen

At "[Healthy Land](https://goo.gl/maps/chHpm6sytsJXvvkb9)" we went to for another Onsen session and as a treat in this location, we got a warm "Sand Bath" on top of it. Sand that is heated up from the volcanic activity (a little only) is shoveled on top of you while you lie in the sand. You stay for 15minutes and enjoy the ride of warmth and quiet and not being able to scratch any of your itches. The Onsen has excellent sea view and beautiful pools to hang out. Once I learned a little about the process to follow in these Onsen, I like the idea and taking a little bit part in this long bathing tradition here. Life seems to happen here. It is cute.

### **Driving** on the road

8 days of driving and not one person has acted erratically of aggressive. That was a fantastic experience for me. It is not my 1st time to drive on the left side of the road, but it is always a little unnerving at the beginning. The speed limits here are anything BUT German. The "Freeways" I have seen the maximum speed limit of 70-80km/h, on the highways ranges from 40-60km/h and in cities, well it is 30-40km/h. Noone seems very much in a rush, and everyone is respectful. People give whenever they can and don't usually take anything, on the road that is. Slow cars pull over as soon as reasonable, no one pulls up to your bumper because he/she thinks you drive 3 km/h too slow. They keep their distance, friendly and safe and wait for you to do the right thing at the right time. Amazing how that can work. Respect!! Respect others, and they will respect you. Give them the benefit of the doubt. Don't get me wrong. I was not happy to spend 3 hours on the road to get a total of 150km on the odometer. The streets are narrow. There are a lot of tight turns a lot of little villages … The German in me was in rebellion mode. But I do see the benefits of attentive driving, for sure.

### Buildings

I am sorry I did not see a lot of ancient buildings in Japan. Not in villages, not in towns, not in cities. It takes away from the character for me. The only reason I can find for this is the fact that Japan exists in really dramatic environmental conditions. It's part of the [RING of FIRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire), has a lot of earthquakes, is surrounded by ocean that seems to produce plenty of [Typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) and then there are the sand storms that come from China "[ASIAN DUST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Dust)". I believe people don't see a reason to invest in really well-built houses for themselves as the risk of it being taken away is so huge every year.
